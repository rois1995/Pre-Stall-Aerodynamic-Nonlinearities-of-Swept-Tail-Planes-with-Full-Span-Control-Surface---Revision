\begin{abstract}
\label{anchor:abstract}
Pre-stall changes in lift growth on swept wings with deflected controls can alter pitching moment before maximum lift. MONNALISA wind-tunnel measurements and Reynolds-averaged Navier--Stokes calculations resolve these changes into fixed-surface and full-span-control loads. For the aft-swept, zero-dihedral model at $\delta=20\degc$, computed lift grows over $\alpha=0\to2\degc$, but by 0.111 less than predicted from its preceding incidence trend. The fixed surface accounts for approximately 63\% of this shortfall despite continuing to gain lift; the control contributes more strongly to the pitching-moment departure about the aerodynamic center. Pressure and spatial-load integrations link the response to stronger leading-edge suction, reduced intermediate- and aft-chord loading, and longer outboard moment arms. With $45\degc$ dihedral, the computed lift increment over the same interval exceeds that without dihedral despite lower absolute lift. The forward-swept planform shows weaker aft lift losses concentrated inboard. Experimental--numerical discrepancies restrict the detailed flow interpretation to the computed solutions.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
A deflected trailing-edge control changes both lift and the location where that load acts. Consequently, the pitching moment can change abruptly even while total lift grows below stall. A local reduction in loading need not coincide with a reduction in integrated lift, because force contributions at different chordwise and spanwise positions combine. Explaining this behavior requires resolving fixed- and moving-surface loads rather than examining integrated coefficients alone.

Plain-flap experiments by Sears and Liddell~\cite{sears1942plain} documented reduced flap effectiveness associated with separation at large deflections. They also showed that similar lift changes can accompany different hinge-moment behavior. On finite rectangular wings with full-span flaps, Campbell et al.~\cite{campbell1956fullspan} related flap chord and aspect ratio to lift and center of pressure. Together these studies establish that flap separation and changes in load location are familiar phenomena, rather than the novelty claimed here.



On swept wings, spanwise changes in local lift can produce pitching-moment nonlinearity before maximum lift~\cite{harper1964sweptstall}. The onset of local stall need not coincide with the change in integrated moment: the force change and its moment arm vary along the span. This motivates analyzing load redistribution instead of assigning a single global stall event.

Swept-flap experiments reinforce this distinction. Tewes et al.~\cite{tewes2014sweepcontrol} found that large deflection could produce separated flap flow with continued lift growth, while sweep altered the associated lift and pitching-moment response. Melton~\cite{melton2014sweptflap} observed spanwise variation in separated flow on a swept wing with a 30\%-chord flap. These lower-Reynolds-number experiments primarily investigated active separation control and cannot validate the present configurations quantitatively. They establish context for the three-dimensional response but do not resolve the fixed- and control-surface contributions.

Reduced-order methods provide another perspective: Sivells and Neely~\cite{sivells1947liftingline} incorporated nonlinear sectional lift and moment data into lifting-line analysis, while Goitia and Llamas~\cite{goitia2019vlm} combined viscous sectional data with vortex-lattice and finite-wing corrections. Such approaches can capture some nonlinear responses, but depend on the supplied sectional representation and corrections. They do not directly expose the pressure and moment-arm redistribution studied here.

The MONNALISA database publication~\cite{rausa2026monnalisa} already documents the baseline pre-stall lift nonlinearity, expansion of reverse flow on the control, associated pressure changes, and continued growth of total lift. Its DUST computations~\cite{tugnoli2021dust} used nonlinear sectional data, while the potential-based calculations were smoother. The present study builds on those observations but addresses a different question: how the loads on the fixed surface and control combine into lift and pitching-moment changes.

An actual increase of fixed-surface lift does not establish that its growth follows the preceding incidence trend; similarly, a control lift loss alone does not establish its contribution to pitching moment. We compare actual increments with those extrapolated from preceding incidence, integrate pressure loads by chord and span, and connect them to skin friction. Integrated WT--RANS comparisons first delimit the interpretation of computed surface loads. The two dihedral orientations and the opposite-sweep planform then reveal how load magnitudes and moment arms influence the response.

The models are isolated tailplane-inspired wings with full-span, 30\%-chord plain controls, without fuselage or wing wake. The results do not establish behavior for partial-span controls or untested geometries.

\label{end:sec:intro}
\section{Methodology}\label{sec:methods}
\subsection{Models and wind-tunnel setup}\label{sec:setup}
Wind-tunnel (WT) measurements provide integrated lift and moment; RANS surface loads resolve their component contributions. Configuration I is aft swept, with $\Gamma=0\degc$ and $45\degc$; configuration II is forward swept and has a different overall planform.

Both wings use a constant symmetric section with thickness ratio $t/c=9\%$, geometric semispan $b=1.6$~m, and a full-span plain control hinged at 70\% chord. Coefficients use the reference semiplanform area $S$ and mean aerodynamic chord $\bar c$, independent of dihedral rotation. Table~\ref{tab:geometry} lists the geometry: quarter-chord sweep $\lambda=+30\degc$ for configuration I and $-15\degc$ for II. Positive $\Gamma$ raises the tip; aspect ratio for the full wing is $\Lambda=2b^2/S$. Configuration II also differs in taper, aspect ratio, area, and chord, so the response cannot be attributed to sweep alone.

\label{end:sec:setup}
\subsection{Conventions and moment references}\label{sec:references}
Positive incidence raises the leading edge, positive control deflection moves the trailing edge downward, and positive pitching moment is nose-up. Body axes are downstream ($x$), spanwise ($y$) and upward ($z$). Forces are normalized by $q_\infty S$; lift and drag follow from the body-axis components:
\begin{equation}
 C_L=C_{F,z}\cos\alpha-C_{F,x}\sin\alpha,\qquad
 C_D=C_{F,z}\sin\alpha+C_{F,x}\cos\alpha,\qquad
 C_{F,k}=F_k/(q_\infty S).
\label{eq:force}
\end{equation}
Pitching moment is normalized by $q_\infty S\bar c$. Body coordinates are expressed relative to the root leading edge.

\label{anchor:fitted_reference}Experimental and numerical aerodynamic centers are fitted separately from zero-deflection moment and body-normal-force trends over $|\alpha|\leq10\degc$ and then fixed as control deflection changes~\cite{rausa2026monnalisa}. For configuration I, WT/RANS positions are $0.6430/0.6420$~m at zero dihedral and $0.6455/0.6435$~m at $45\degc$, differences of about 1 and 2~mm. For II, the positions are $-0.0492/-0.00362$~m, differing by 45.6~mm. Thus matching the reference construction does not impose identical moment axes on WT and RANS; the common-axis dihedral diagnostic below explicitly uses one physical reference.

The original numerical reference is $O=(0.228571,0,0)$~m, the root quarter-chord of Geom.~I. For a numerical pole transported from abscissa $x_0$ to $x_1$,
\begin{equation}
 C_M(x_1)=C_M(x_0)+\frac{x_1-x_0}{\bar c}C_{F,z}.
\label{eq:transport}
\end{equation}
Table~\ref{tab:geometry} lists the fitted abscissae. Matched dihedral diagnostics use the identical numerical axis $A_I=(0.642000,0,0)$~m in both configurations, denoted $C_M^{A_I}$. Geom.~II uses its own MAC once for every moment contribution; all coefficients use the fixed areas in Table~\ref{tab:geometry}.

\label{end:sec:references}
\subsection{Numerical setup}\label{sec:numerics}
SU2~\cite{palacios2013stanford} solves compressible ideal-gas RANS with Sutherland viscosity, Spalart--Allmaras turbulence~\cite{spalart1992sa}, and the Bas--Cakmakcioglu transition model~\cite{cakmakcioglu2020bcm}. Vertex-centered median-dual cells use Roe fluxes, second-order MUSCL reconstruction~\cite{van1979towards}, Green--Gauss gradients and a Venkatakrishnan limiter~\cite{venkatakrishnan1993accuracy} (coefficient 0.05); turbulence convection uses first-order upwinding.

\label{anchor:wall_model}The wing has no-slip, adiabatic walls, freestream far-field and root symmetry boundaries. Steady pseudo-time iteration uses implicit Euler, FGMRES and adaptive CFL. The lift/drag Cauchy stopping tolerance is approximately $3\times10^{-6}$ over 100 entries.

Hex-dominant, octree-based meshes~\cite{rausa2026monnalisa} resolve the leading edge, hinge junction, trailing edge, and incidence-dependent wake. Boundary-layer extrusion has nominal first-cell height $2\times10^{-6}$~m and growth ratio 1.15. Representative median wall distance is $y^+=0.19$--0.26, with maxima 1.1--1.3.

A four-level grid study on configuration I used $\delta=35\degc$, $\Gamma=0,45\degc$ and $\alpha=0,18\degc$ (Table~\ref{tab:mesh}, Fig.~\ref{fig:mesh}). Nominal meshes had 9.7--12.7 million points versus 50.1--65.9 million on the finest level. Moments in Fig.~\ref{fig:mesh} use the original numerical pole.

Nominal-to-finest differences were 1.20--2.14\% in lift and 1.13--2.49\% in drag. At $\Gamma=0\degc$, $\alpha=18\degc$, $C_M^O$ changed from $-0.813$ to $-0.827$; the $45\degc$ moment sequence was nonmonotonic. The nominal mesh reduced campaign cost but gives no discretization-error bound for local component loads.

\subsection{Load decomposition and trend departures}\label{sec:analysis}
An actual coefficient increment is its change over a sampled incidence interval. Its departure subtracts the increment predicted by continuing an empirically fitted preceding slope over the same interval. At $\delta=20\degc$, $\alpha=0\to2\degc$, for example, fixed-surface lift increases by $+0.0593$ rather than the predicted $+0.1285$: its departure is $-0.0692$. A negative departure therefore means reduced growth relative to the chosen reference, not necessarily an actual loss of lift.

We integrate pressure and viscous loads over fixed, control and tip surfaces using consistent reference quantities. The control includes the hinge junction and blunt trailing edge; tip loads remain in total coefficients.

For lift or moment coefficient $Q$, the actual increment and departure of component $j$ are
\begin{equation}
 \Delta C_{Q,j}=C_{Q,j}(\alpha_b)-C_{Q,j}(\alpha_a),\qquad
 D_{Q,j}=\Delta C_{Q,j}-m_{Q,j}^{\mathrm{pre}}(\alpha_b-\alpha_a).
\label{eq:departure}
\end{equation}
Each preceding slope is an unweighted least-squares fit using identical incidence samples for all load components, preserving additivity. For $\delta=20\degc$ the primary preceding range is $\alpha\in\{-6,-4,-2\}\degc$; for the early $\delta=30\degc$ feature it is $\{-16,-14,-12\}\degc$. These empirical slopes are not assumed to represent attached flow. Neighboring fitting windows quantify reference sensitivity, while finite differences remain meaningful when the preceding trend is curved.

Propagating the supplied measured-coefficient uncertainties through $D=\sum_i w_i C_i$ gives
\begin{equation}
 u_{\mathrm{ind}}=\left[\sum_i(w_i u_i)^2\right]^{1/2},\qquad
 u_{\mathrm{L1}}=\sum_i|w_i|u_i.
\label{eq:uncertainty}
\end{equation}
The first assumes independent errors; the second gives an absolute-weight bound. Unknown inter-incidence covariance and coverage prevent interpreting either as a confidence interval.

Pressure and wall-shear forces are integrated on the triangulated surface over chordwise and spanwise regions. The pressure coefficient is $C_p=(p-p_\infty)/q_\infty$ and wall-shear coefficient $\mathbf C_f=\boldsymbol\tau_w/q_\infty$. The moment about reference abscissa $x_A$ is
\begin{equation}
 C_M(x_A)=\frac{1}{q_\infty S\bar c}
 \int\big[z\,dF_x-(x-x_A)dF_z\big].
\label{eq:moment}
\end{equation}
The terms $z\,dF_x$ and $-(x-x_A)dF_z$ are retained separately for common-axis dihedral comparisons. Integrated force and moment reconstructions close against solver totals.

Chordwise regions are forward ($\xi<0.4$), intermediate ($0.4\leq\xi<0.7$), and aft ($\xi\geq0.7$), with $\xi$ the local chord fraction after straightening the control about its hinge. The aft region is further divided into spanwise thirds, using $\eta=s/b$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:regions}). Integrals cover both physical sides, with hinge junction and end-face loads included in the appropriate totals. Pressure cuts at $\eta=0.2,0.5,0.8$ follow the actual surface intersections without smoothing or bridging gaps; the reported regional coefficients are integrated loads, not densities.

\begin{figure}[htbp]\centering\includegraphics[width=.72\textwidth]{figures_revision_load_regions.pdf}
\caption{Reference planform: chordwise integration regions, spanwise thirds, hinge, and pressure-section locations.}\label{fig:regions}\end{figure}

\label{anchor:reversal_definition}For each surface face, a downstream local chord direction $\mathbf d_c$ is rotated with the control, then projected into the surface tangent plane as $\mathbf t_c=[\mathbf d_c-(\mathbf d_c\cdot\mathbf n)\mathbf n]/\|\mathbf d_c-(\mathbf d_c\cdot\mathbf n)\mathbf n\|$. Thus $C_{f,c}=\mathbf C_f\cdot\mathbf t_c$ measures the chordwise component of wall shear in the local, dihedral- and flap-aware orientation. End faces are excluded from this shear diagnostic.

The fraction $f_{\mathrm{rev}}$ is the upper-control area fraction ($\xi>0.7$, excluding end faces) with $C_{f,c}<0$. A value of 0.5 means that upstream-directed chordwise shear occupies half the control's upper surface. It indicates near-wall reversal, not a complete three-dimensional separation footprint~\cite{tobak1982topology,mccormack1949sweptforward}.

\section{Load Breakdown of the Reference Model}\label{sec:joint}
\subsection{Fixed-surface and control-surface loads}\label{sec:budget}
The reference analysis concerns configuration I at zero dihedral and $\delta=20\degc$. In Fig.~\ref{fig:budget}, the upper panels show the fixed, control, and total coefficients. Dashed segments continue each preceding fitted slope, anchored at its actual zero-incidence value, across the shaded $\alpha=0\to2\degc$ interval. The endpoint gap is the departure; the lower signed markers distinguish the actual increment from that departure. The two displays answer different questions: whether a surface gains load, and how its growth differs from the preceding trend.

\begin{figure}[htbp]\centering\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures_revision_baseline_budget.pdf}
\caption{Fixed-surface, control-surface, and total numerical loading, with actual increments and departures from preceding incidence trends.}\label{fig:budget}\end{figure}

The fixed surface actually gains $+0.0593$ in lift coefficient, while the control loses $-0.0351$. Together, they yield a positive total increase of $+0.0242$. Continued growth of total lift is therefore sustained by the fixed-surface gain exceeding the control loss. Nevertheless, the fixed-surface gain is smaller than expected from its preceding slope, and both surfaces contribute negative departures: $-0.0692$ for fixed and $-0.0414$ for control, for a total of $-0.1106$ (Table~\ref{tab:budget}). The reduction in lift growth therefore involves both the fixed surface and the control.

The fixed-surface share of approximately 63\% is the ratio $D_{L,\mathrm{fixed}}/D_{L,\mathrm{total}}$, using these two negative departures. It measures each surface's share of the computed lift-growth shortfall. Across the tested preceding-incidence ranges, this fraction remains 62.4--62.6\%, while the total lift departure varies from approximately $-0.105$ to $-0.137$ (Appendix~\ref{app:sensitivity}). The amplitude depends on the empirical preceding trend, but its division between surfaces is comparatively stable.

Tip loads contribute less than 0.09\% of the changes listed in Table~\ref{tab:budget} and are included in the totals. The dihedral load decomposition retains their appreciable contribution explicitly.

At $\alpha=0\degc$, $\delta=20\degc$, the computed lift coefficient is approximately 0.301 lower with $\Gamma=45\degc$. Projection of the reference panel-normal loading onto the common lift direction accounts algebraically for about 0.247 of this difference; changes in local loading and the spanwise-force contribution supply the remainder. Pitching moment depends on where $F_x$ and $F_z$ act. Dihedral places the loading above the reference axis, so $F_x$ contributes through the vertical arm $z$. The $F_z$ contribution is weighted by the downstream arm $x-x_A$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:moment}). Both numerical solutions are referred to the same physical axis $A_I$. Maggin and Shanks~\cite{maggin1946dihedral} showed why this choice matters: for a 40$\degc$ swept-back wing, moments about fixed reference axes indicated an apparent increase in longitudinal stability with geometric dihedral, whereas only a slight change remained when each configuration was referred to its own mean aerodynamic chord. The common axis therefore compares the loading and its moment arms about one physical point, whereas Table~\ref{tab:geometry_budget} uses each configuration's computed aerodynamic center. Figure~\ref{fig:dihedral}(a,b) compares their moment increments through
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal I_M=[C_{M,45}^{A_I}(\alpha_b)-C_{M,45}^{A_I}(\alpha_a)]
             -[C_{M,0}^{A_I}(\alpha_b)-C_{M,0}^{A_I}(\alpha_a)]
\label{eq:interval}
\end{equation}
A negative $\mathcal I_M$ means that the moment increase is smaller, or the decrease larger, with dihedral over the same incidence interval. These are differences of actual moment increments, not departures from preceding fitted trends.

Over $\alpha=-4\to-2\degc$, it contributes $-0.00362$, partly offset by a $+0.00131$ contribution from $F_z$, leaving $\mathcal I_M=-0.00231$. Across $\alpha=0\to2\degc$, the terms instead reinforce: $-0.01283$ from $F_x$ and $-0.02750$ from $F_z$ give $-0.04033$. The $F_z$ moment term supplies the larger difference during this reference loading change. Over $\alpha=2\to4\degc$, its contribution falls to $-0.00260$, while the contribution from $F_x$ remains $-0.01412$ and dominates the $-0.01672$ total. A single relative weighting of the two moment terms therefore cannot describe the sequence.

At $\delta=30\degc$, the terms reinforce over $\alpha=-2\to0\degc$: $-0.00808$ from $F_x$ and $-0.01027$ from $F_z$ give a total $-0.01836$. Over $\alpha=0\to2\degc$, both remain negative, with the $F_x$ contribution larger. Over $\alpha=2\to4\degc$, a positive difference from $F_z$ of $+0.00556$ offsets part of the $-0.01286$ difference from $F_x$, leaving $-0.00730$. This change from reinforcement to compensation concerns the moment-term differences between geometries; it is distinct from fixed/control moment-departure reinforcement in Sec.~\ref{sec:budget}.

The next step is to establish whether these moment changes accompany expansion of reverse flow on the control or loading changes elsewhere on the model. At $\delta=30\degc$, the upper-control reverse-flow area fraction is already 0.889 at $\alpha=-2\degc$, increasing only to 0.911 and 0.912 at $\alpha=0\degc$ and $\alpha=2\degc$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:reversal_geometry}). This combination motivates examining the fixed-surface pressure and skin friction over the same incidence sequence.

Sweep is the principal geometric difference. Area and MAC differ by approximately 4\% and 8\%, respectively, and taper changes from 0.4 to 0.6 (Table~\ref{tab:geometry}); these accompanying differences prevent attributing the response to sweep alone.

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}
The wind-tunnel measurements show that reductions in pre-stall lift growth and changes in pitching moment do not necessarily occur over the same incidence interval or at the same control deflection. Resolving the computed forces into fixed- and control-surface contributions, then examining their spatial distribution, identifies how different combinations of loading changes produce the numerical lift and pitching responses.

The reference computation shows that continued total lift growth can conceal reduced growth on both the fixed surface and the full-span control. For configuration I at zero dihedral and $\delta=20\degc$, fixed lift gain exceeds actual control lift loss, yet the fixed surface accounts for approximately 63\% of the total departure below the preceding trend. The control contributes more strongly to the positive pitching-moment departure about the numerical aerodynamic center.

Pressure sections and regional integrations explain why stronger leading-edge suction is consistent with reduced fixed-surface growth: the forward-region gain falls below its preceding trend, while the intermediate region actually loses lift. Similar aft lift losses produce larger positive moment increments outboard as their downstream moment arms grow. The upper-control shear pattern shifts from reverse flow adjacent to the hinge to reverse flow near the trailing edge across the computed interval. Its subsequent upstream extension coexists with renewed total lift growth. Consequently, the extent of reverse flow complements the pressure and load evidence but does not determine the integrated response by itself.

The measurements establish distinct lift and moment responses across deflection and geometry. RANS reproduces the principal reference trends with a displaced lift interval and substantial coefficient-level bias, while correspondence is weaker for the dihedral and forward-swept cases. The detailed loading findings therefore characterize the computed flow fields and loads.

For matched dihedral, adjacent lift increments reverse their ordering between the two orientations. The common-axis moment comparison also changes from reinforcement to compensation between the contributions from $F_x$ and $F_z$ across incidence intervals. Both the force magnitudes and their moment arms enter this incidence-dependent response.

The forward-swept planform gives stronger fixed-surface lift growth, much weaker control loss, positive intermediate-chord growth and an inboard-concentrated aft lift loss over the matched $\alpha=0\to2\degc$ interval. Consequently, its aft moment increments lack the reference model's increase in positive moment contribution toward the tip about the corresponding aerodynamic centers. This connects the different pitching responses to the chordwise and spanwise organization of the computed loading.

Future computations on the finer levels of the established mesh hierarchy are planned to assess the resolution sensitivity of the pre-stall load redistribution, with particular attention to pitching moment and the fixed- and control-surface contributions. The present joint analysis identifies how continued lift growth can coexist with different component lift deficits and pitching-moment contributions.

\begin{figure}[htbp]\centering\includegraphics[width=.80\textwidth]{figures_revision_partition_check.pdf}
\caption{Alternative integration partitions: spanwise thirds with fore/aft regions (left), and quarters with forward/intermediate/aft regions (right).}\label{fig:partition_check}\end{figure}
\FloatBarrier

Moving the spanwise boundary from one third to one quarter changes the inboard aft departure from approximately $-0.0127$ to $-0.00939$, while complete fore/aft sums remain unchanged. Finer chord subdivisions preserve the two negative fixed-chord departures. These checks support the complete loading interpretation while showing that individual regional contributions depend on the partition.

The main text compares the regional loads with the near-wall flow on the control, measured by the reverse-flow area fraction. Replacing the zero threshold with small negative values, $C_{f,c}<-10^{-6}$ and $C_{f,c}<-10^{-5}$, leaves the reference result nearly unchanged. With the larger threshold, the $\delta=20\degc$ fraction grows from 0.145 to 0.480 across $\alpha=0\to2\degc$, compared with 0.146 to 0.485 in Fig.~\ref{fig:reversal}. These threshold checks and the fractions over other geometric partitions are retained in the reproducibility record; the main figures use $C_{f,c}<0$.

\label{anchor:saved_state}The same measure shows a feature of the stored $\Gamma=45\degc$ solutions that is not interpreted. The upper control surface reverse-flow area fractions decrease between $\alpha=2\degc$ and $\alpha=4\degc$, from about 0.58 to 0.09 at $\delta=20\degc$ and from 0.91 to 0.20 at $\delta=30\degc$. Their physical origin remains unresolved, so we do not interpret them as a transition or reattachment sequence.

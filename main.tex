\begin{abstract}
\label{anchor:abstract}
Local loading changes on a swept lifting surface with a deflected plain control can alter lift growth and pitching moment well before maximum lift. MONNALISA wind-tunnel measurements and Reynolds-averaged Navier--Stokes calculations are used to examine how fixed-surface and full-span control loads combine in this response. For the positive-sweep, zero-dihedral model at a $20\degc$ deflection, the computed fixed surface continues gaining lift while both surface contributions fall below their preceding incidence trends. Across the tested baseline windows and zero-incidence solutions, the fixed surface accounts for about 63\% of the total lift departure; the control contributes more strongly to the moment departure about the fitted aerodynamic center. Pressure sections and regional integrations connect these different allocations to stronger leading-edge suction, reduced hinge/aft loading, and larger outboard moment weights. Matched dihedral comparisons distinguish absolute load projection from changes with incidence. The forward-swept planform gives weaker aft losses and a different spanwise moment allocation. The detailed interpretation concerns the computed states, for which correspondence with measured integrated coefficients is partial. The combined force and moment analysis identifies loading changes that continuing total lift growth alone would conceal.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
A deflected trailing-edge control changes both the amount of lift generated by a lifting surface and the location at which that load acts. Its response with incidence is therefore relevant to pitching moment as well as lift, even before the maximum-lift condition is reached. When local loading changes, total lift can continue increasing while its growth slows and the pitching response changes more abruptly. Interpreting this behavior requires relating the integrated coefficients to the loads on the fixed and moving parts of the surface.

Sectional plain-flap experiments establish the starting point for this problem. Sears and Liddell~\cite{sears1942plain} measured lift, drag, pitching moment, and hinge moment for a 30\%-chord plain flap on an NACA 0015 airfoil, including sealed and unsealed hinge gaps. Separation reduced flap effectiveness at large deflections, although additional deflection still increased lift within the tested range. Their comparison with a thinner symmetric section also showed that similar lift effectiveness could coexist with different hinge-moment behavior. Reduced flap effectiveness is thus established physics; neither the presence of separation nor a single integrated lift trend fully characterizes the loading response.

On a finite wing, flap-induced loading is also shaped by the spanwise flow and planform. Campbell, Blanks, and Leaver~\cite{campbell1956fullspan} investigated rectangular wings of small aspect ratio with full-span plain flaps of several chord fractions. Their force and pitching-moment measurements, compared with thin-wing theory and empirical thickness and boundary-layer allowances, related flap effectiveness and center of pressure to flap chord and aspect ratio. Those unswept, thinner, transition-tripped models differ from the present swept models, but they establish why the location of the additional load matters alongside its magnitude.

For swept wings, Harper and Maki~\cite{harper1964sweptstall} documented how spanwise changes in local lift response can produce pitching-moment nonlinearity below maximum lift. The first appearance of local stall need not coincide with the first integrated moment change, because the force change and its moment arm vary along the span. Their broad stall review provides context for examining load redistribution, rather than an equivalent test of the present deflected-control configurations.

Nonlinear sectional data provide one route for representing these responses in reduced-order models. Sivells and Neely~\cite{sivells1947liftingline} coupled such lift data to lifting-line theory and also calculated wing pitching moment from sectional moment data. Goitia and Llamas~\cite{goitia2019vlm} coupled a vortex-lattice formulation to viscous sectional data, with effective-sweep and finite-wing corrections, to predict nonlinear wing and tail lift. Consequently, the ability of a reduced-order model to reproduce the response depends on how sectional nonlinearities and three-dimensional loading changes are represented.

The immediate basis for the present work is the MONNALISA database publication~\cite{rausa2026monnalisa}. Its Sec.~IV and Fig.~11 already document the baseline pre-stall lift nonlinearity, expansion of reversed flow on the control, associated pressure changes, and continued growth of total lift. Its DUST formulation uses sectional tables and corrections, while the retained potential-based panel and vortex-lattice predictions are smoother. The present loading analysis builds on these established observations.

The remaining loading question is how changes on the fixed surface and control combine to produce the joint lift and moment response. Continued fixed-surface lift growth, for example, does not establish that this contribution follows its preceding trend. Comparing ordinary increments with departures from that trend identifies which components supply the reduced total growth, while spatial pressure integrations show why the component ranking can differ for moment. This study develops that quantitative interpretation from the existing Reynolds-averaged Navier--Stokes (RANS) solutions, first assessing their integrated coefficients against the measured response and its supplied uncertainty. Changes in control deflection and geometry then test how broadly the reference loading pattern persists within the dataset.

The test articles are isolated tailplane-inspired wings with full-span, 30\%-chord plain controls; no fuselage, wing wake, or complete-aircraft trim condition is represented. The $45\degc$ dihedral setting supplies a pronounced orientation contrast on the same planform, while a forward-swept planform provides the contrasting sweep direction. These comparisons define the scope of the analysis without establishing behavior for partial-span controls, other flap chords, or intermediate dihedral angles.

\label{end:sec:intro}

\subsection{Models and experimental installation}\label{sec:setup}

To examine dihedral, the reference aft-swept planform (configuration I) is investigated at $\Gamma=0\degc$ and $45\degc$, with its in-plane geometry unchanged. A forward-swept planform (configuration II) provides a contrast in sweep direction and overall planform response. The labels I and II are used in the tables.

Both models use a symmetric section of constant profile along the undeformed semispan $b=1.6$~m, with thickness ratio $t/c=9\%$, where $c$ is the local chord. Each model has a full-span plain control hinged at 70\% chord. The reference surface $S$ is the undeformed semiplanform area, and $\bar c$ denotes its mean aerodynamic chord (MAC). Configuration II also differs in taper, aspect ratio, MAC and chord-based Reynolds number, so the sweep contrast is interpreted through the overall planform response.

\subsection{Conventions and moment references}\label{sec:references}
Positive incidence $\alpha$ raises the leading edge relative to the freestream, and positive control deflection $\delta$ moves the trailing edge downward relative to the fixed section. Lift is positive toward the upper surface and pitching moment is positive nose-up. The WT and numerical data are expressed in these common physical conventions. In body axes, $x$ is downstream, $y$ is spanwise, and $z$ is upward. The body-force components $F_k$, with $k=x,y,z$, are normalized by the freestream dynamic pressure $q_\infty=\rho_\infty U_\infty^2/2$ and area $S$, where $\rho_\infty$ and $U_\infty$ are freestream density and speed. Lift and drag coefficients follow from
\begin{equation}
 C_L=C_{F,z}\cos\alpha-C_{F,x}\sin\alpha,\qquad
 C_D=C_{F,z}\sin\alpha+C_{F,x}\cos\alpha,\qquad
 C_{F,k}=F_k/(q_\infty S).
\label{eq:force}
\end{equation}
Pitching moment is normalized by $q_\infty S\bar c$. Body coordinates are expressed relative to the root leading edge.

\label{anchor:fitted_reference}Both experimental and numerical moments are referred to aerodynamic centers determined from the zero-deflection loading characteristics. Linear fits to moment and body-normal force over $|\alpha|\leq10\degc$ define the positions, which remain fixed when control deflection varies~\cite{rausa2026monnalisa}. The experimental fits use the supplied uncertainty weights; the numerical fits use the retained zero-deflection linear trends. For configuration I, the WT and RANS positions are 0.6430 and 0.6420~m at zero dihedral, and 0.6455 and 0.6435~m at $45\degc$: separations of about 1.0 and 2.0~mm. For configuration II they are $-0.0492$ and $-0.00362$~m, separated by 45.6~mm. The comparison coefficient $\CM$ therefore uses the same reference construction, with each dataset's fitted position; it is not an imposed quarter-MAC moment.

The original numerical reference is $O=(0.228571,0,0)$~m, the root quarter-chord of Geom.~I. For a numerical pole transported from abscissa $x_0$ to $x_1$,
\begin{equation}
 C_M(x_1)=C_M(x_0)+\frac{x_1-x_0}{\bar c}C_{F,z}.
\label{eq:transport}
\end{equation}
The fitted abscissae are in Table~\ref{tab:geometry}. Matched dihedral diagnostics use the identical numerical axis $A_I=(0.642000,0,0)$~m in both configurations, denoted $C_M^{A_I}$. Geom.~II uses its own MAC once for every moment contribution; all coefficients use the fixed areas in Table~\ref{tab:geometry}.

\label{end:sec:references}
\subsection{Numerical methodology}\label{sec:numerics}
The SU2~\cite{palacios2013stanford} calculations solve compressible ideal-gas RANS with Sutherland viscosity, the Spalart--Allmaras (SA) turbulence model~\cite{spalart1992sa}, and the Bas--Cakmakcioglu (BCM) algebraic transition treatment~\cite{cakmakcioglu2020bcm}. The finite-volume mean-flow discretization uses median-dual vertex cells and Roe fluxes with second-order monotonic upstream-centered schemes for conservation laws (MUSCL) reconstruction~\cite{van1979towards}, Green--Gauss gradients, and a Venkatakrishnan limiter~\cite{venkatakrishnan1993accuracy} with coefficient 0.05. SA convection uses first-order scalar upwinding. The BCM intermittency factor $\gamma_{\mathrm{BC}}$ modifies turbulence production without an additional transported transition equation.

\label{anchor:wall_model}The model surfaces have no-slip, adiabatic boundaries, with freestream conditions at the far field and symmetry at the root. The symmetry plane omits the physical support and boundary-layer development over the raised-floor arrangement. Steady solutions use implicit Euler pseudo-time advancement and flexible generalized minimal residual (FGMRES) linear solves, with adaptive Courant--Friedrichs--Lewy (CFL) number. The production stopping criterion monitors lift and drag over 100 entries with a Cauchy tolerance of approximately $3\times10^{-6}$. Case-specific solution sensitivity is discussed where it affects the interpretation.

The surface grid resolves the leading edge, hinge junction, and trailing edge, where curvature and pressure changes require additional resolution. Wall-normal boundary-layer extrusion uses a nominal first-cell height of about $2\times10^{-6}$~m and growth ratio 1.15, while incidence-aware volume refinement follows the wake. The volume mesh is hex-dominant and octree-based~\cite{rausa2026monnalisa}. Representative solutions have median nondimensional wall distance $y^+=0.19$--0.26, with local maxima about 1.1--1.3. A reference grid contains approximately 9.44 million points and 18.92 million volume elements.

The existing grid-refinement assessment uses four levels---coarse, nominal, fine, and very fine---for configuration I at $\delta=35\degc$, $\Gamma=0,45\degc$, and $\alpha=0,18\degc$. Table~\ref{tab:mesh} gives the point and element ranges. Figure~\ref{fig:mesh} compares lift, drag, and pitching moment against the actual grid-point count on a logarithmic scale; open squares identify the nominal level, and lines connect the retained levels. Moments are about the numerical origin $O$, using the configuration-I area and MAC.

The nominal level was selected to balance spatial resolution with the demands of the extensive MONNALISA campaign across geometries, incidences, and control deflections. Its approximately 9.7--12.7 million points compare with 50.1--65.9 million on the finest level. Nominal-to-finest differences are 1.20--2.14\% for lift and 1.13--2.49\% for drag. At zero dihedral and $\alpha=18\degc$, $C_M^O$ changes from approximately $-0.813$ to $-0.827$, an absolute difference of 0.0134; the inclined moment sequence is nonmonotonic. The assessment supports the nominal grid as a practical production compromise while showing finite, condition-dependent integrated-load sensitivity. It provides no discretization-error bound for local component loads. The comparison with measurements in Sec.~\ref{sec:response} separately assesses reproduction of the principal pre-stall aerodynamic trends.

\subsection{Additive loading and empirical departures}\label{sec:analysis}
A surface can gain lift while contributing to a slowdown in total lift growth. To distinguish these effects, the actual increment is the measured or computed coefficient change between two incidences. The expected increment is obtained by linearly extrapolating the empirically fitted preceding slope over the same interval. Their difference, actual minus expected, is the departure. For the fixed surface of configuration I at zero dihedral and $\delta=20\degc$, the actual increase from $0$ to $2\degc$ is $+0.0593$, while continuation of the preceding trend predicts $+0.1285$. The departure is $-0.0692$: the fixed surface still gains lift, but gains 0.0692 less than predicted by that trend. This is a reduction in growth, not an actual lift loss.

The analysis resolves these changes into additive surface loads. Solver surface groups define the fixed surface (upper/lower fixed panels), the control (upper/lower control panels, hinge junction, and blunt trailing edge), and tip. Pressure and viscous contributions are retained on both physical sides with the same global $S$ and $\bar c$. Tip loads remain in every total and closure check. All displayed coefficients use their actual magnitude.

For coefficient label $Q=L$ or $M$ and surface contribution $j$ (fixed, control, tip, or total), the ordinary increment is the change between sampled incidences $\alpha_a$ and $\alpha_b$. Its departure subtracts the change predicted by continuing the preceding slope $m_{Q,j}^{\mathrm{pre}}$, measured per degree,
\begin{equation}
 \Delta C_{Q,j}=C_{Q,j}(\alpha_b)-C_{Q,j}(\alpha_a),\qquad
 D_{Q,j}=\Delta C_{Q,j}-m_{Q,j}^{\mathrm{pre}}(\alpha_b-\alpha_a).
\label{eq:departure}
\end{equation}
The preceding slope is an unweighted linear least-squares fit to the declared preceding samples. Using identical samples and weights for every component and the total preserves additivity for both increments and departures. The primary preceding-incidence range for $\delta=20\degc$ is $\{-6,-4,-2\}\degc$; the early reference $\delta=30\degc$ numerical baseline is $\{-16,-14,-12\}\degc$. These are empirical references, with no assumption that the preceding flow is attached. Neighboring preceding-incidence fitting ranges and alternate zero-incidence solutions test this reference dependence; their effects are reported alongside the corresponding loading results. Where the preceding sequence is curved, signed finite differences describe the response without imposing a unique event amplitude.

The uncertainty sensitivity of each measured lift or moment departure is evaluated by propagating the supplied coefficient uncertainties through its defining linear combination. Equation~\ref{eq:departure} is written as $D=\sum_i w_iC_i$, where $i$ indexes the measured samples, $w_i$ is the combined coefficient weight, and $u_i$ is its supplied propagated uncertainty magnitude. The independence-based sensitivity $u_{\mathrm{ind}}$ and absolute-weight sensitivity $u_{\mathrm{L1}}$ are
\begin{equation}
 u_{\mathrm{ind}}=\left[\sum_i(w_i u_i)^2\right]^{1/2},\qquad
 u_{\mathrm{L1}}=\sum_i|w_i|u_i.
\label{eq:uncertainty}
\end{equation}
The first is conditional on independence; the second bounds a perturbation when each sample varies within its supplied magnitude. Coverage and between-incidence covariance are undocumented, so these are contrast sensitivities rather than confidence intervals.

To locate the changes identified by the component budget, pressure and wall-shear forces are integrated over chordwise and spanwise regions. The pressure coefficient is $C_p=(p-p_\infty)/q_\infty$, and the skin-friction vector is $\mathbf C_f=\boldsymbol\tau_w/q_\infty$, where $p_\infty$ is freestream pressure and $\boldsymbol\tau_w$ is wall shear. Linear surface tractions and their moments are integrated over the triangulated surface, with
\begin{equation}
 C_M(x_A)=\frac{1}{q_\infty S\bar c}
 \int\big[z\,dF_x-(x-x_A)dF_z\big].
\label{eq:moment}
\end{equation}
Here $x_A$ is the moment-reference abscissa, and $dF_x,dF_z$ are local force contributions. The axial-force term $z\,dF_x$ and normal-force term $-(x-x_A)dF_z$ are retained separately for the common-axis dihedral comparison. Reconstructed full-surface forces and moments agree with the solver-integrated coefficients before decomposition into chordwise and spanwise load contributions; detailed closure checks are retained in the reproducibility records.

The regions distinguish loading upstream of the hinge from loading on the deflected control, and compare inboard, middle, and outboard contributions. The nondimensional span position is $\eta=s/b$, where $s$ is distance along the undeformed span; $\xi$ is the local chord fraction after straightening the control about its hinge. Chordwise regions are forward ($\xi<0.4$), intermediate ($0.4\leq\xi<0.7$), and aft ($\xi\geq0.7$). Chordwise contributions are summed over the complete semispan, while the aft spanwise contributions use semispan thirds (Fig.~\ref{fig:regions}). Contributions include both physical sides, with end-face loads completing the total. Sectional $C_p$ is shown at $\eta=0.2,0.5,0.8$ on both physical sides. The reference sections use the fixed and control panels, excluding narrow hinge-junction and end faces; the dihedral and forward-swept sections use complete surface intersections, including the junction and blunt trailing edge. Curves follow the connected surface intersections without bridging gaps or smoothing. Regional contributions are integrated loads, rather than load densities.

\caption{Undeformed reference planform: chordwise integration regions, spanwise thirds, hinge, and pressure-section locations.}\label{fig:regions}\end{figure}

\label{anchor:reversal_definition}The local downstream direction must account for control rotation and surface orientation before wall-shear reversal is assessed. Let $\mathbf d_c$ be the downstream chord direction: $(1,0,0)$ on the fixed panel, rotated about the physical hinge on the control. On each surface triangle with unit normal $\mathbf n$, its tangent-plane projection is normalized to give $\mathbf t_c=[\mathbf d_c-(\mathbf d_c\cdot\mathbf n)\mathbf n]/\|\mathbf d_c-(\mathbf d_c\cdot\mathbf n)\mathbf n\|$. This construction points downstream on both physical sides, includes dihedral and flap rotation, and is applied after transforming symmetry-reflected states into the common physical convention. End faces are excluded from the reversal measure; the retained upper-control faces have no degenerate projected directions.

The signed coefficient $C_{f,c}=\mathbf C_f\cdot\mathbf t_c$ is negative where the chordwise component of wall shear points upstream. We report the fraction $f_{\mathrm{rev}}$ of the upper side of the deflected control ($\xi>0.7$, excluding end faces) satisfying $C_{f,c}<0$, weighted by actual surface area. Thus $f_{\mathrm{rev}}=0.5$ means that upstream-directed chordwise shear occupies half that upper-control area. Linear interpolation within each triangle locates the sign crossing. This is an indicator of reversed near-wall chordwise flow; it need not cover the entire three-dimensional separated footprint and does not identify laminar--turbulent transition.

The absolute lift difference has a substantial force-projection component. At $\alpha=0\degc$, $\delta=20\degc$, the computed $C_L$ values are 0.8422 and 0.5407 for $\Gamma=0$ and $45\degc$, respectively, a difference of $-0.3015$. Rotating the reference zero-dihedral normal load into the lift direction accounts for $-0.2467$ of this difference. This decomposition states how the force components combine at a fixed incidence. It does not establish that the normal loading remains unchanged when the model is rotated.

The second comparison reverses quarter-chord sweep from $+30\degc$ to $-15\degc$ while retaining the 1.6~m semispan and full-span 30\%-chord control. Sweep is the principal geometric contrast. \section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}
The reference computation shows that continued total lift growth can conceal reduced growth on both the fixed surface and the full-span control. For configuration I at zero dihedral and $\delta=20\degc$, fixed lift gain exceeds actual control lift loss, yet the fixed surface accounts for approximately 63\% of the total departure below the preceding trend. The control contributes more strongly to the positive pitching-moment departure about the numerical aerodynamic center.

Pressure sections and regional integrations explain why stronger leading-edge suction is consistent with reduced fixed-surface growth: the forward-region gain falls below its preceding trend, while the intermediate region actually loses lift. Similar aft lift losses produce larger positive moment increments outboard as their downstream moment arms grow. The upper-control shear pattern changes from a hinge-adjacent reversed band to trailing-edge-region reversal across the computed interval. Its subsequent upstream extension coexists with renewed total lift growth. Reversed-shear extent consequently complements the pressure and load evidence without determining the integrated response by itself.

The measurements establish distinct lift and moment responses across deflection and geometry. RANS reproduces the principal reference directions with a displaced lift interval and substantial coefficient-level bias, while correspondence is weaker for the dihedral and forward-swept cases. The detailed loading findings therefore characterize the computed states.

For matched dihedral, a large part of the absolute lift reduction is an algebraic force-projection effect, but adjacent lift increments reverse their ordering between the two orientations. The common-axis moment comparison also changes from reinforcement to compensation of axial- and normal-force contrasts across incidence intervals. A constant projection factor does not describe this joint response.

The forward-swept planform gives stronger fixed-surface lift growth, much weaker control loss, positive intermediate-chord growth and an inboard-concentrated aft lift loss over the matched $0\to2\degc$ interval. Its aft moment increments consequently lack the reference model's increasing positive outboard progression about the corresponding aerodynamic centers. This is a computed planform-dependent redistribution, with simultaneous geometry and Reynolds-number differences, rather than a sweep-only law.

Future computations on the finer levels of the established mesh hierarchy are planned to assess the resolution sensitivity of the pre-stall load redistribution, with particular attention to pitching moment and the fixed- and control-surface contributions. The present joint analysis identifies how continued lift growth can coexist with different surface deficits and pitching-moment weights.

\section{Regional and Saved-State Checks}\label{app:sensitivity}
Figure~\ref{fig:partition_check} shows the two partitions used to check the regional interpretation. The first divides the semispan into thirds and the chord at the hinge; the second uses spanwise quarters and adds a chord boundary at $\xi=0.4$.

\begin{figure}[htbp]\centering\includegraphics[width=.80\textwidth]{partition_check.pdf}
\caption{Alternative integration partitions: spanwise thirds with fore/aft regions (left), and quarters with forward/intermediate/aft regions (right).}\label{fig:partition_check}\end{figure}

Moving the spanwise boundary from one third to one quarter changes the inboard aft departure from approximately $-0.0127$ to $-0.00939$, while complete fore/aft sums remain unchanged. Finer chord subdivisions preserve the two negative fixed-chord departures. These checks support the complete loading interpretation while showing that individual bin magnitudes depend on the partition.

Using II's symmetry-reflected zero state gives a $0\to2\degc$ lift increase of 0.1121 rather than the direct 0.0935, and a moment departure of 0.0070 rather than 0.0146. These remain separate steady solutions, rather than averaged values or a hysteresis measurement.

For reference $\delta=20\degc$, the zero-incidence reversed-shear fractions are 0.14578 direct and 0.14089 reflected. Small-threshold and geometric-partition checks are retained in the reproducibility record; the main figure uses only $C_{f,c}<0$.

\label{anchor:saved_state}At $45\degc$ dihedral, the stored upper-control reversed-shear fractions decrease between $2$ and $4\degc$, from about 0.58 to 0.09 at $\delta=20\degc$ and from 0.91 to 0.20 at $30\degc$. Their physical origin is unresolved, so they are not interpreted as a transition or reattachment sequence. The integrated $20\degc$ lift irregularity over $-6\to-4\degc$ exceeds the retained terminal variations of order $10^{-4}$, but this does not establish a unique steady state.

The surface fields and their associated solver force reports support the adopted load accounting. Terminal-history registration remains incomplete for the reference $\delta=30\degc$, $\alpha=-6\degc$ state and for II at $\delta=20\degc$, $\alpha=-4\degc$ and direct $0\degc$. The central reference $20\degc$ result uses matched states; the II regional comparison retains this state-provenance limitation. Detailed source and recovery checks are kept with the reproducibility material.

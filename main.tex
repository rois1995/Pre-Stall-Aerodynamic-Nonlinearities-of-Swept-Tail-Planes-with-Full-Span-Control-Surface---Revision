\begin{abstract}
\label{anchor:abstract}
Local loading changes on a swept lifting surface with a deflected plain control can alter lift growth and pitching moment well before maximum lift. MONNALISA wind-tunnel (WT) measurements and Reynolds-averaged Navier--Stokes (RANS) solutions are used to examine how fixed-surface and full-span control loads combine in this response. For the positive-sweep, zero-dihedral model at a $20\degc$ deflection, the computed fixed surface continues gaining lift while both surface contributions fall below their preceding incidence trends. Across the tested baseline windows and zero-incidence solutions, the fixed surface accounts for about 63\% of the total lift departure; the control contributes more strongly to the moment departure about the fitted aerodynamic center. Pressure sections and regional integrations connect these different allocations to stronger leading-edge suction, reduced hinge/aft loading, and larger outboard moment weights. Matched dihedral comparisons distinguish absolute load projection from changes with incidence. The detailed interpretation concerns the computed states, for which correspondence with measured integrated coefficients is partial. The combined force and moment analysis identifies loading changes that continuing total lift growth alone would conceal.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
A deflected trailing-edge control changes both the amount of lift generated by a lifting surface and the location at which that load acts. Its response with incidence is therefore relevant to pitching moment as well as lift, even before the maximum-lift condition is reached. When local loading changes, total lift can continue increasing while its growth slows and the pitching response changes more abruptly. Interpreting this behavior requires relating the integrated coefficients to the loads on the fixed and moving parts of the surface.

Sectional plain-flap experiments establish the starting point for this problem. Sears and Liddell~\cite{sears1942plain} measured lift, drag, pitching moment, and hinge moment for a 30\%-chord plain flap on an NACA 0015 airfoil, including sealed and unsealed hinge gaps. Separation reduced flap effectiveness at large deflections, although additional deflection still increased lift within the tested range. Their comparison with a thinner symmetric section also showed that similar lift effectiveness could coexist with different hinge-moment behavior. Reduced flap effectiveness is thus established physics; neither the presence of separation nor a single integrated lift trend fully characterizes the loading response.

On a finite wing, flap-induced loading is also shaped by the spanwise flow and planform. Campbell, Blanks, and Leaver~\cite{campbell1956fullspan} investigated rectangular wings of small aspect ratio with full-span plain flaps of several chord fractions. Their force and pitching-moment measurements, compared with thin-wing theory and empirical thickness and boundary-layer allowances, related flap effectiveness and center of pressure to flap chord and aspect ratio. Those unswept, thinner, transition-tripped models differ from the present swept models, but they establish why the location of the additional load matters alongside its magnitude.

For swept wings, Harper and Maki~\cite{harper1964sweptstall} documented how spanwise changes in local lift response can produce pitching-moment nonlinearity below maximum lift. The first appearance of local stall need not coincide with the first integrated moment change, because the force change and its moment arm vary along the span. Their broad stall review provides context for examining load redistribution, rather than an equivalent test of the present deflected-control configurations.

Nonlinear sectional data provide one route for representing these responses in reduced-order models. Sivells and Neely~\cite{sivells1947liftingline} coupled such lift data to lifting-line theory and also calculated wing pitching moment from sectional moment data. Goitia and Llamas~\cite{goitia2019vlm} coupled a vortex-lattice formulation to viscous sectional data, with effective-sweep and finite-wing corrections, to predict nonlinear wing and tail lift. Consequently, the ability of a reduced-order model to reproduce the response depends on how sectional nonlinearities and three-dimensional loading changes are represented.

The immediate basis for the present work is the MONNALISA database publication~\cite{rausa2026monnalisa}. Its Sec.~IV and Fig.~11 already document the baseline pre-stall lift nonlinearity, expansion of reversed flow on the control, associated pressure changes, and continued growth of total lift. Its DUST formulation uses sectional tables and corrections, while the retained potential-based panel and vortex-lattice predictions are smoother. The present loading analysis builds on these established observations.

The remaining loading question is how changes on the fixed surface and control combine to produce the joint lift and moment response. Continued fixed-surface lift growth, for example, does not establish that this contribution follows its preceding trend. Comparing ordinary increments with departures from that trend identifies which components supply the reduced total growth, while spatial pressure integrations show why the component ranking can differ for moment. This study develops that quantitative interpretation from the existing Reynolds-averaged Navier--Stokes (RANS) solutions, first assessing their integrated coefficients against the measured response and its supplied uncertainty. Changes in control deflection and geometry then test how broadly the reference loading pattern persists within the dataset.

The test articles are isolated tailplane-inspired wings with full-span, 30\%-chord plain controls; no fuselage, wing wake, or complete-aircraft trim condition is represented. The $45\degc$ dihedral setting supplies a pronounced orientation contrast on the same planform, while a forward-swept planform provides the contrasting sweep direction. These comparisons define the scope of the analysis without establishing behavior for partial-span controls, other flap chords, or intermediate dihedral angles.

\label{end:sec:intro}

\subsection{Models and experimental installation}\label{sec:setup}

To examine dihedral, the reference aft-swept planform (configuration I) is investigated at $\Gamma=0\degc$ and $45\degc$, with its in-plane geometry unchanged. A forward-swept planform (configuration II) provides a contrast in sweep direction and overall planform response. The labels I and II are used in the tables.

Both models use a symmetric section of constant profile along the undeformed semispan $b=1.6$~m, with thickness ratio $t/c=9\%$, where $c$ is the local chord. Each model has a full-span plain control hinged at 70\% chord. The reference surface $S$ is the undeformed semiplanform area, and $\bar c$ denotes its mean aerodynamic chord (MAC). Configuration II also has a different taper and aspect ratio, so the sweep contrast is interpreted through the overall planform response.

\subsection{Conventions and moment references}\label{sec:references}
Positive incidence $\alpha$ raises the leading edge relative to the freestream, and positive control deflection $\delta$ moves the trailing edge downward relative to the fixed section. Lift is positive toward the upper surface and pitching moment is positive nose-up. The WT and numerical data are expressed in these common physical conventions. In body axes, $x$ is downstream, $y$ is spanwise, and $z$ is upward. The body-force components $F_k$, with $k=x,y,z$, are normalized by the freestream dynamic pressure $q_\infty=\rho_\infty U_\infty^2/2$ and area $S$, where $\rho_\infty$ and $U_\infty$ are freestream density and speed. Lift and drag coefficients follow from
\begin{equation}
 C_L=C_{F,z}\cos\alpha-C_{F,x}\sin\alpha,\qquad
 C_D=C_{F,z}\sin\alpha+C_{F,x}\cos\alpha,\qquad
 C_{F,k}=F_k/(q_\infty S).
\label{eq:force}
\end{equation}
Pitching moment is normalized by $q_\infty S\bar c$. Body coordinates are expressed relative to the root leading edge.

\label{anchor:fitted_reference}Both experimental and numerical moments are referred to aerodynamic centers determined from the zero-deflection loading characteristics. Linear fits to moment and body-normal force over $|\alpha|\leq10\degc$ define the positions, which remain fixed when control deflection varies~\cite{rausa2026monnalisa}. The experimental fits use the supplied uncertainty weights; the numerical fits use the retained zero-deflection linear trends. For configuration I, the WT and RANS positions are 0.6430 and 0.6420~m at zero dihedral, and 0.6455 and 0.6435~m at $45\degc$: separations of about 1.0 and 2.0~mm. For configuration II they are $-0.0492$ and $-0.00362$~m, separated by 45.6~mm. The comparison coefficient $\CM$ therefore uses the same reference construction, with each dataset's fitted position; it is not an imposed quarter-MAC moment.

The original numerical reference is $O=(0.228571,0,0)$~m, the root quarter-chord of Geom.~I. For a numerical pole transported from abscissa $x_0$ to $x_1$,
\begin{equation}
 C_M(x_1)=C_M(x_0)+\frac{x_1-x_0}{\bar c}C_{F,z}.
\label{eq:transport}
\end{equation}
The fitted abscissae are in Table~\ref{tab:geometry}. Matched dihedral diagnostics use the identical numerical axis $A_I=(0.642000,0,0)$~m in both configurations, denoted $C_M^{A_I}$. Geom.~II uses its own MAC once for every moment contribution; all coefficients use the fixed areas in Table~\ref{tab:geometry}.

\label{end:sec:references}
\subsection{Numerical method and existing accuracy evidence}\label{sec:numerics}
The SU2~\cite{palacios2013stanford} calculations solve compressible ideal-gas RANS with Sutherland viscosity, the Spalart--Allmaras (SA) turbulence model~\cite{spalart1992sa}, and the Bas--Cakmakcioglu (BCM) algebraic transition treatment~\cite{cakmakcioglu2020bcm}. The finite-volume mean-flow discretization uses median-dual vertex cells and Roe fluxes with second-order monotonic upstream-centered schemes for conservation laws (MUSCL) reconstruction~\cite{van1979towards}, Green--Gauss gradients, and a Venkatakrishnan limiter~\cite{venkatakrishnan1993accuracy} with coefficient 0.05. SA convection uses first-order scalar upwinding. The BCM intermittency factor $\gamma_{\mathrm{BC}}$ modifies turbulence production without an additional transported transition equation.

\label{anchor:wall_model}The model surfaces have no-slip, adiabatic boundaries, with freestream conditions at the far field and symmetry at the root. The symmetry plane omits the physical support and boundary-layer development over the raised-floor arrangement. Steady solutions use implicit Euler pseudo-time advancement and flexible generalized minimal residual (FGMRES) linear solves, with adaptive Courant--Friedrichs--Lewy (CFL) number. The production stopping criterion monitors lift and drag over 100 entries with a Cauchy tolerance of approximately $3\times10^{-6}$. Case-specific solution sensitivity is discussed where it affects the interpretation.

The surface grid resolves the leading edge, hinge junction, and trailing edge, where curvature and pressure changes require additional resolution. Wall-normal boundary-layer extrusion uses a nominal first-cell height of about $2\times10^{-6}$~m and growth ratio 1.15, while incidence-aware volume refinement follows the wake. The volume mesh is hex-dominant and octree-based~\cite{rausa2026monnalisa}. Representative solutions have median nondimensional wall distance $y^+=0.19$--0.26; local maxima are about 1.1--1.3, so the median does not imply that every point has $y^+<1$. Production grids contain on the order of $10^7$ volume points. A reference grid contains approximately 9.44 million points and 18.92 million volume elements.

The existing grid-refinement assessment uses four levels---coarse, nominal, fine, and very fine---for configuration I at $\delta=35\degc$, $\Gamma=0,45\degc$, and $\alpha=0,18\degc$. Table~\ref{tab:mesh} gives the point and element ranges. Figure~\ref{fig:mesh} compares lift, drag, and pitching moment against the actual grid-point count on a logarithmic scale; open squares identify the nominal level, and lines connect the retained levels. Moments are about the numerical origin $O$, using the configuration-I area and MAC.

The nominal level was selected to balance spatial resolution with the demands of the extensive MONNALISA campaign across geometries, incidences, and control deflections. Its approximately 9.7--12.7 million points compare with 50.1--65.9 million on the finest level. Nominal-to-finest differences are 1.20--2.14\% for lift and 1.13--2.49\% for drag. At zero dihedral and $\alpha=18\degc$, $C_M^O$ changes from approximately $-0.813$ to $-0.827$, an absolute difference of 0.0134; the inclined moment sequence is nonmonotonic. The assessment supports the nominal grid as a practical production compromise while showing finite, condition-dependent integrated-load sensitivity. It provides no discretization-error bound for local component loads. The comparison with measurements in Sec.~\ref{sec:response} separately assesses reproduction of the principal pre-stall aerodynamic trends.

\subsection{Additive loading and empirical departures}\label{sec:analysis}
A total lift increase can conceal reduced growth of one or more surface contributions. The analysis therefore compares the actual coefficient change with the change obtained by continuing an empirical preceding trend, then resolves both measures into additive loads. Solver surface groups define the fixed surface (upper/lower fixed panels), the control (upper/lower control panels, hinge junction, and blunt trailing edge), and tip. Pressure and viscous contributions are retained on both physical sides with the same global $S$ and $\bar c$. Tip is included in every total, and all component coefficients are displayed at their actual magnitude.

For coefficient label $Q=L$ or $M$ and surface contribution $j$ (fixed, control, tip, or total), the ordinary increment is the change between sampled incidences $\alpha_a$ and $\alpha_b$. Its departure subtracts the change predicted by continuing the preceding slope $m_{Q,j}^{\mathrm{pre}}$, measured per degree,
\begin{equation}
 \Delta C_{Q,j}=C_{Q,j}(\alpha_b)-C_{Q,j}(\alpha_a),\qquad
 D_{Q,j}=\Delta C_{Q,j}-m_{Q,j}^{\mathrm{pre}}(\alpha_b-\alpha_a).
\label{eq:departure}
\end{equation}
The preceding slope is an unweighted linear least-squares fit to the declared preceding samples. Using identical samples and weights for every component and the total preserves additivity for both increments and departures. The primary preceding-incidence range for $\delta=20\degc$ is $\{-6,-4,-2\}\degc$; the early reference $\delta=30\degc$ numerical baseline is $\{-16,-14,-12\}\degc$. These are empirical references, with no assumption that the preceding flow is attached. Neighboring preceding-incidence fitting ranges and alternate zero-incidence solutions test this reference dependence (Appendix~\ref{app:sensitivity}). Where the preceding sequence is curved, signed finite differences describe the response without imposing a unique event amplitude.

Measured departures must also be assessed against the supplied uncertainty. For WT contrasts, Eq.~\ref{eq:departure} is written as $D=\sum_i w_iC_i$, where $i$ indexes the measured samples, $w_i$ is the combined coefficient weight, and $u_i$ is its supplied propagated uncertainty magnitude. The independence-based sensitivity $u_{\mathrm{ind}}$ and absolute-weight sensitivity $u_{\mathrm{L1}}$ are
\begin{equation}
 u_{\mathrm{ind}}=\left[\sum_i(w_i u_i)^2\right]^{1/2},\qquad
 u_{\mathrm{L1}}=\sum_i|w_i|u_i.
\label{eq:uncertainty}
\end{equation}
The first is conditional on independence; the second bounds a perturbation when each sample varies within its supplied magnitude. Neither is an established confidence interval or a new experimental uncertainty budget. Covariance-dependent significance is not inferred.

To locate the changes identified by the component budget, pressure and wall-shear forces are integrated over chordwise and spanwise regions. The pressure coefficient is $C_p=(p-p_\infty)/q_\infty$, and the skin-friction vector is $\mathbf C_f=\boldsymbol\tau_w/q_\infty$, where $p_\infty$ is freestream pressure and $\boldsymbol\tau_w$ is wall shear. Linear surface tractions and their moments are integrated over the triangulated surface, with
\begin{equation}
 C_M(x_A)=\frac{1}{q_\infty S\bar c}
 \int\big[z\,dF_x-(x-x_A)dF_z\big].
\label{eq:moment}
\end{equation}
Here $x_A$ is the moment-reference abscissa, and $dF_x,dF_z$ are local force contributions. The axial-force term $z\,dF_x$ and normal-force term $-(x-x_A)dF_z$ are retained separately for the common-axis dihedral comparison. Reconstructed full-surface forces and moments are checked against the solver-integrated coefficients before regional partitioning. Their maximum lift and moment differences are below $10^{-4}$; the detailed closure and effect-scale checks remain in the reproducibility records.

The regions distinguish loading upstream of the hinge from loading on the deflected control, and compare inboard, middle, and outboard contributions. The nondimensional span position is $\eta=s/b$, where $s$ is distance along the undeformed span; $\xi$ is the local chord fraction after straightening the control about its hinge. Chordwise regions are forward ($\xi<0.4$), intermediate ($0.4\leq\xi<0.7$), and aft ($\xi\geq0.7$). Spanwise regions divide the semispan into thirds. Contributions include both physical sides, with end-face loads completing the total. Sectional $C_p$ is shown at $\eta=0.2,0.5,0.8$ on both sides of the fixed and control panels, excluding the hinge junction and end faces. Regional contributions are integrated loads, rather than load densities.

\label{anchor:reversal_definition}Local reverse flow is identified by wall shear directed upstream. We report the fraction $f_{\mathrm{rev}}$ of the actual upper aft-surface area ($\xi\geq0.7$, excluding end faces) for which $\mathbf C_f\cdot\mathbf t_c<-\epsilon$. The signed chordwise coefficient is $C_{f,c}=\mathbf C_f\cdot\mathbf t_c$, where the unit vector $\mathbf t_c$ points downstream along the local chord in the surface tangent plane; $\epsilon=0$ gives the primary definition. Small positive thresholds test near-zero sensitivity in the appendix. This indicator quantifies reverse chordwise wall shear, rather than the complete separated area in three-dimensional flow.

The lower-deflection result shows why the two coefficients should be examined together. At $10\degc$, the measured moment increment changes from $-0.0038$ over $-2\to0\degc$ to $+0.011$ over $0\to2\degc$, so a moment-response change is present below the deflection producing the pronounced $20\degc$ lift slowdown.

Increasing deflection to $30\degc$ instead gives a broader early feature: lift increments are 0.077, 0.064, and 0.111 over $-12\to-10$, $-10\to-8$, and $-8\to-6\degc$, while moment already increases by about 0.018 on each of the first two intervals. Curvature in the preceding lift sequence makes its departure window-sensitive, spanning both signs. Local increments therefore describe this feature more directly than one departure amplitude.

Introducing $45\degc$ positive dihedral changes the joint response. For the reference configuration with $45\degc$ dihedral at $\delta=20\degc$, measured lift growth remains suppressed across $-2\to2\degc$, but the positive moment increment over $-2\to0\degc$ has a departure smaller than both uncertainty sensitivities. At $\delta=30\degc$, the selected lift slowdown occurs over $-2\to0\degc$, whereas moment decreases earlier, by 0.027 over $-4\to-2\degc$. That moment departure is comparable with $u_{\mathrm{ind}}$ and smaller than $u_{\mathrm{L1}}$. Thus coincident, clearly resolved lift and moment events cannot be assumed for the inclined model.

The second planform provides a further contrast in the joint response. For the forward-swept configuration at $\delta=20\degc$, measured lift grows by 0.044 over $0\to2\degc$, compared with approximately 0.092 on the preceding and following intervals, and moment increases by 0.019. The primary moment departure exceeds both sensitivities, although results obtained using neighboring preceding-incidence fitting ranges do not uniformly exceed the primary L1 sensitivity. Its lift and moment changes are locally evident, with the $30\degc$ response also shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:comparison_planform} for context.

\label{anchor:comparison}To assess what the surface fields can explain, the numerical response is compared separately for lift and moment. For the reference configuration at $\delta=20\degc$, the RANS lift slowdown occurs over $0\to2\degc$, one sampled interval later than in WT. The numerical zero-incidence lift coefficients are approximately 0.842--0.843, compared with WT 0.556 and uncertainty magnitude 0.013. A qualitatively similar slowdown and less-negative moment response therefore coexist with substantial lift-level bias. At $30\degc$, the early numerical lift change is sharper than the broad measured feature, and its fitted-center moment departure is window-sensitive.

At $\Gamma=45\degc$, numerical $\delta=20\degc$ lift decreases over $-6\to-4\degc$ while WT increases; the computed sequence does not reproduce the sustained measured slowdown or weak moment response convincingly. At $\delta=30\degc$, a negative moment change is present, with the strongest numerical bracket later than the measured $-4\to-2\degc$ interval. For the forward-swept configuration at $\delta=20\degc$, the direct numerical $0\to2\degc$ lift increment is 0.094, more than twice WT 0.044. The corresponding fitted-center moment increment, 0.017, is locally close to WT 0.019, retaining the distinct reference constructions.

The reference calculation reproduces the direction of the joint response, whereas correspondence is weaker for the inclined and forward-swept cases. The numerical load decomposition is therefore used to investigate how changes in surface pressure and the development of locally reversed flow contribute to the computed lift and pitching-moment response.

\subsection{Ordinary growth and reduced growth of the fixed surface}\label{sec:budget}
For the reference RANS sequence, the continuing total lift increase is supplied by a gain on the fixed surface that outweighs a loss on the control (Fig.~\ref{fig:budget} and Table~\ref{tab:budget}). \subsection{Control deflection and separated-flow development}\label{sec:reversal}
Increasing control deflection tests whether the reference distinction between ordinary growth and reduced growth persists in the earlier nonlinear interval. The reference $\delta=30\degc$ results in Table~\ref{tab:budget} again combine positive ordinary fixed lift growth with negative fixed and control departures. The preceding range for this early interval is $\{-16,-14,-12\}\degc$. Its fitted-center moment contributions compete, however, and the total departure changes sign across tested windows. The lift-budget pattern extends to this deflection, while its moment response is more sensitive to the empirical preceding trend.

The associated wall-shear measure relates this budget to the changing aft pressure and loading. For the reference configuration at $\delta=20\degc$, the upper-aft reverse-flow fraction rises from 0.146 to 0.485 over $0\to2\degc$, with local reverse flow already present at $-2\degc$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:reversal}). At $\delta=30\degc$, it rises from 0.622 to 0.935 over $-10\to-8\degc$. Expansion of the existing reverse-flow region accompanies reduced aft loading; its area does not represent every form of three-dimensional separation. This growth persists under the near-zero wall-shear thresholds examined in Appendix~\ref{app:sensitivity}.

Positive dihedral changes both the projection of aerodynamic forces and the spatial arms through which they contribute to pitching moment. The comparison holds planform, incidence, deflection, area, and MAC fixed, with the associated root cut changing with dihedral. Figure~\ref{fig:geometry_measured} overlays the measured configurations at identical deflections, making the changes in coefficient level and incidence response directly visible.

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}
The reference calculation shows that continued fixed-surface lift growth can coexist with reduced growth of both the fixed and control contributions. At zero dihedral and a $20\degc$ deflection, about 63\% of the computed lift departure relative to the selected preceding trends is allocated to the fixed surface. The control contributes more strongly to the pitching-moment departure about the fitted center. Stronger leading-edge suction together with reduced intermediate/aft loading, and larger outboard moment arms, connect this different allocation to the spatial loading changes.

The measured response establishes why lift and moment must be considered together: the pronounced $20\degc$ lift slowdown is accompanied by a less-negative moment, while a moment-response change is already present at $10\degc$. The earlier $30\degc$ lift feature is broader, and the weak inclined moment departures are not clearly resolved above both uncertainty sensitivities.

Matched dihedral comparisons distinguish changes in force projection from changes in incidence-dependent moment contributions. The forward-swept calculation also gives reinforcing fitted-center moment departures, extending the component result to that tested planform.

RANS reproduces the principal reference trends with a displaced lift interval and substantial coefficient-level bias; correspondence is weaker for the inclined and forward-swept responses. Future computations on the finer levels of the established mesh hierarchy are planned to assess the resolution sensitivity of the pre-stall load redistribution, with particular attention to pitching moment and the fixed- and control-surface contributions. The reference result nevertheless identifies a joint loading response that total lift growth alone would conceal.

The complete fore/aft sums persist when span thirds are replaced by quarters, and finer chord subdivisions preserve the two negative fixed-chord departures. Typical stored vector skin-friction norms are of order $10^{-3}$, so the small thresholds assess near-zero sensitivity without a statistical uncertainty interpretation.

\subsection{Moment-reference effects}

\subsection{Computed-state sensitivity}
\label{anchor:saved_state}The inclined reverse-flow fractions are nonmonotonic: between $2$ and $4\degc$ they fall from approximately 0.58 to 0.09 at $\delta=20\degc$, and from 0.91 to 0.20 at $\delta=30\degc$.

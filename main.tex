\begin{abstract}
\label{anchor:abstract}Pre-stall lift and pitching-moment changes on swept tailplane models with full-span plain controls are examined using MONNALISA wind-tunnel measurements and Reynolds-averaged Navier--Stokes solutions. The analysis distinguishes coefficient increments from departures relative to preceding incidence trends. For the positive-sweep, zero-dihedral model at a $20\degc$ deflection, computed fixed-surface lift continues increasing but grows more slowly than the preceding trend. The fixed surface accounts for about 63\% of the total lift departure across the tested baseline windows and zero-incidence solutions, whereas the control contributes more strongly to the moment departure about the fitted aerodynamic center. Sectional pressures and spatial load integrations relate this difference to increased leading-edge suction, weaker hinge/aft loading, and different spanwise moment weights. Matched dihedral comparisons quantify changes in load projection and moment contributions with incidence. Agreement with measured integrated coefficients is partial, so the detailed loading interpretation concerns the computed states. Continuing lift growth can thus coexist with reduced growth of both component contributions and a different component ranking in pitching moment.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
Separation and reduced effectiveness at large plain-flap deflections are established features of flapped lifting surfaces~\cite{sears1942plain}. On swept finite wings, planform and three-dimensional flow also influence load distribution and stall progression~\cite{harper1964sweptstall}. Full-span plain controls have been studied on finite wings: Campbell, Blanks, and Leaver~\cite{campbell1956fullspan} measured lift, drag, and pitching moment on low-aspect-ratio rectangular models with several flap chords.

The aerodynamic question considered here is how local loading changes combine in the integrated pre-stall response. A component can continue gaining lift while falling below its preceding growth trend. Comparing ordinary increments with departures from that trend quantifies this distinction. Pitching moment additionally weights where the changes occur: similar force changes at different chordwise or spanwise locations can produce different moment contributions.

The MONNALISA database publication~\cite{rausa2026monnalisa}, Sec.~IV and Fig.~11, documents the baseline lift nonlinearity, expanding control-surface reversed flow, pressure changes, and continued growth of total lift, as well as nonlinear lower-fidelity behavior. The present study extends that account by quantifying how changes on the fixed and control surfaces combine in lift and pitching moment, where the changes occur, and how they differ with deflection and dihedral. Measured lift and moment responses are characterized separately to establish the experimental context for the numerical loading interpretation.

The test articles are isolated tailplane-inspired wings, without a fuselage, wing wake, or complete-aircraft trim condition. Their full-span, 30\%-chord controls define the tested extent; partial-span controls and other flap chords are outside the dataset. The $45\degc$ dihedral setting provides a large change in orientation on the same planform. It is a strong geometric contrast, rather than a typical conventional-tail setting. A second planform changes several geometric parameters together.

Nonlinear lifting-line methods can incorporate sectional viscous data~\cite{sivells1947liftingline}. In MONNALISA, the DUST lifting-line formulation uses sectional tables and corrections and gives nonlinear responses, while the retained potential-based panel and vortex-lattice predictions are smoother~\cite{rausa2026monnalisa}. The available behavior is therefore model-specific. The RANS solutions provide the surface loads needed for the present analysis; their integrated lift and moment are first assessed against the measurements.

\label{end:sec:intro}

\begin{equation}
 C_L=C_{F,z}\cos\alpha-C_{F,x}\sin\alpha,\qquad
 C_D=C_{F,z}\sin\alpha+C_{F,x}\cos\alpha,\qquad

Pitching moment is positive nose-up and normalized by $q_\infty S\bar c$. Table~\ref{tab:mapping} summarizes the retained angle and frame mapping. No further angle flip or alignment shift is applied to improve agreement.

\subsection{Numerical method and existing accuracy evidence}\label{sec:numerics}
The existing SU2~\cite{palacios2013stanford} solutions use compressible ideal-gas RANS at the nominal WT Mach and Reynolds conditions, with Sutherland viscosity. Unstructured finite volumes use median-dual vertex control volumes. Mean-flow convection uses Roe fluxes with second-order MUSCL reconstruction~\cite{van1979towards}, Green--Gauss gradients, and a Venkatakrishnan limiter~\cite{venkatakrishnan1993accuracy} with coefficient 0.05. SA~\cite{spalart1992sa} convection uses scalar upwinding without MUSCL, hence first-order reconstruction. The BCM transition closure~\cite{cakmakcioglu2020bcm} supplies an algebraic intermittency correction, not an independently transported transition equation. Spatial order must therefore be distinguished between mean-flow and SA equations.

\label{anchor:wall_model}The surfaces have no-slip, zero-heat-flux boundaries; the exterior uses a far-field boundary and the root plane a symmetry boundary. This excludes the WT floor, its layer, and the support. Implicit Euler pseudo-time advancement, FGMRES linear solves, adaptive CFL, and staged restarts were used to obtain steady states. The configured Cauchy stopping criterion monitors lift and drag over 100 entries, commonly at $3\times10^{-6}$ and at $10^{-6}$ in later Geom.~II stages. Moment is not a stopping quantity; iteration caps and restarts also occur.

Terminal histories match the force reports for 41 of the 44 interpreted surface states; state-specific exceptions and incomplete final-stage registration are detailed in Appendix~\ref{app:sensitivity}. For the inclined $\delta=20\degc$ irregularity over $-6\to-4\degc$, last-100 lift ranges are approximately $10^{-4}$, much smaller than the between-state change. These iterative checks complement the mesh and experiment--model comparisons.

The automated mesh procedure~\cite{rausa2026monnalisa} uses anisotropic surface triangles, leading-edge and hinge/junction refinement, a hex-dominant octree volume, and incidence-oriented wake refinement. Nominal controls are a $2.5\degc$ leading-edge angle, $1\degc$ junction angle, seven trailing-edge points, maximum surface spacing 0.02~m, and boundary-layer growth ratio 1.15. Actual first-cell height and layer count are not registered for every retained mesh. Representative stored $y^+$ medians are 0.26 for I/$\delta=20\degc$/$\alpha=2\degc$, 0.19 for I/$\Gamma=45\degc$/$\delta=30\degc$/$\alpha=0\degc$, and 0.23 for II/$\delta=20\degc$/$\alpha=0\degc$, with maxima approximately 1.22, 1.26, and 1.13. These support resolved near-wall treatment for those samples.

Selected surface meshes have 122,860 points/182,423 elements for I/$\delta=20\degc$ at $0,2,4\degc$, 141,828/209,641 for I/$\Gamma=45\degc$/$\delta=30\degc$ at $-2,0,2\degc$, and 105,506/156,333 for II/$\delta=20\degc$ at $0,2,4\degc$. These are surface counts. The retained volume for the I/$\alpha=0\degc$/$\delta=-20\degc$ parent contains 9,440,030 points and 18,918,212 volume elements; equivalent registered volume counts are not available for every interpreted state.

Table~\ref{tab:mesh} gives grid-size ranges for the existing four-level study, and Fig.~\ref{fig:mesh} shows its coefficient behavior. It concerns Geom.~I at $\delta=35\degc$, $\Gamma=0,45\degc$, and $\alpha=0,18\degc$. The published lift/drag assessment is augmented only by moments linked to the same retained states. Nominal-to-finest differences are 1.20--2.14\% for lift and 1.13--2.49\% for drag. The moment gap at $\Gamma=0\degc$, $\alpha=18\degc$ is 0.01343; the inclined sequence is nonmonotonic. This is inherited integrated-load sensitivity evidence, without an asymptotic-order or discretization-uncertainty estimate. It does not establish convergence of the current $20/30\degc$ departures, regional loads, pressure sections, or separation topology, nor of Geom.~II.

\subsection{Additive loading and empirical departures}\label{sec:analysis}
Solver surface groups define the fixed surface (upper/lower fixed panels), the control (upper/lower control panels, hinge junction, and blunt trailing edge), and tip. Pressure and viscous contributions are retained on both physical sides with the same global $S$ and $\bar c$. Tip is included in every total; no component is multiplied for display.

For coefficient $Q$ and component $j$, the ordinary increment is the change between two sampled incidences. Its departure from the preceding trend subtracts the increment predicted by continuation of that trend:
\begin{equation}
 \Delta C_{Q,j}=C_{Q,j}(\alpha_b)-C_{Q,j}(\alpha_a),\qquad
 D_{Q,j}=\Delta C_{Q,j}-m_{Q,j}^{\mathrm{pre}}(\alpha_b-\alpha_a).
\label{eq:departure}
\end{equation}
The preceding slope is an unweighted linear least-squares fit to the declared actual samples. All additive components and the total use identical samples and weights, preserving their sum. The primary $\delta=20\degc$ baseline is $\{-6,-4,-2\}\degc$; the early I/$\delta=30\degc$ numerical baseline is $\{-16,-14,-12\}\degc$. Each is an empirical reference, without an assumption that the preceding flow is attached. Adjacent windows and the direct and symmetry-reflected zero-incidence solutions test sensitivity. Curved sequences are described using signed finite differences rather than a forced unique event amplitude.

For WT contrasts, Eq.~\ref{eq:departure} is written as $D=\sum_i w_iC_i$, combining repeated uses of any sample into one weight. The two reported sensitivities are
\begin{equation}
 u_{\mathrm{ind}}=\left[\sum_i(w_i u_i)^2\right]^{1/2},\qquad
 u_{\mathrm{L1}}=\sum_i|w_i|u_i.
\label{eq:uncertainty}
\end{equation}
The first is conditional on independence; the second bounds a perturbation when each sample varies within its supplied magnitude. Neither is an established confidence interval or a new experimental uncertainty budget. Covariance-dependent significance is not inferred.

Saved nodal $C_p$ and vector $\mathbf C_f$ are integrated over the complete triangulated surface. Linear traction and position-times-traction are integrated exactly within each triangle, with
\begin{equation}
 C_M(x_A)=\frac{1}{q_\infty S\bar c}
 \int\big[z\,dF_x-(x-x_A)dF_z\big].
\label{eq:moment}
\end{equation}
The two terms are retained separately where a common-axis dihedral comparison needs them. All 44 full-surface reconstructions have endpoint residuals below $1.5\times10^{-4}$. Maximum lift and moment residuals are $2.71\times10^{-5}$ and $7.06\times10^{-5}$. Conservative absolute-weight residual bounds are checked separately against each increment or departure, with a 10\% effect-scale limit for an adopted interpretation. For the baseline lift departure, the bound is $1.01\times10^{-5}$ against $|D_L|=0.11060$. Small shear changes that fail their corresponding bound are not assigned a resolved mechanism. Accounting closure and reconstruction agreement are distinct from iteration, mesh sensitivity, and experiment--model discrepancy; global residuals do not bound each regional error.

Geometric bins use $\eta=s/b$ and straightened $\xi$, obtained by undoing the control rotation about the three-dimensional hinge. They include both sides and root extensions; end faces are retained separately. Whole triangles are assigned by centroid. These geometric regions differ from the solver surface groups, especially in their assignment of the hinge junction. Sectional pressure curves use $\eta=0.2,0.5,0.8$ and preserve actual contour connectivity on the native upper/lower fixed and control panels, excluding junction and end faces. Three sections illustrate pressure changes; complete integrations supply the budgets.

\label{anchor:reversal_definition}A chordwise reversal indicator measures the actual upper geometric-aft area ($\xi\geq0.7$, excluding end caps) on which $\mathbf C_f\cdot\mathbf t_c<-\epsilon$, with downstream chord direction projected into the face tangent. Linear nodal signs are clipped within triangles. The fraction $f_{\mathrm{rev}}$ is tested at $\epsilon=0,10^{-6},10^{-5}$. It measures a specified component of reversed shear, rather than the full three-dimensional separation footprint.

\label{anchor:uncertainty}For I/$\Gamma=0\degc$/$\delta=20\degc$, the measured lift slope decreases sharply over $-2\to0\degc$: lift grows by 0.0199, compared with 0.0892 over $-4\to-2\degc$. At $\delta=30\degc$, the early measured lift increments are 0.0765, 0.0642, and 0.1113 over $-12\to-10$, $-10\to-8$, and $-8\to-6\degc$. At the lower $10\degc$ deflection, the moment increment changes from $-0.0038$ over $-2\to0\degc$ to $+0.0113$ over $0\to2\degc$. Thus the measured moment response changes at a lower deflection than the pronounced $20\degc$ lift slowdown.

For I/$\Gamma=45\degc$/$\delta=20\degc$, measured lift growth remains suppressed across $-2\to2\degc$. The moment increment over $-2\to0\degc$ is positive, but its departure is smaller than both uncertainty sensitivities. For II/$\delta=20\degc$, measured lift grows by 0.0440 over $0\to2\degc$, compared with approximately 0.0921 on the preceding and following intervals. Moment increases by 0.0187. The $30\degc$ response is also shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:comparison} for context.

\label{anchor:comparison}The numerical correspondence differs between coefficients. For I/$\delta=20\degc$, the RANS lift slowdown occurs over $0\to2\degc$, one sampled interval later than in WT. At zero incidence the direct and reflected numerical lift coefficients are approximately 0.8422 and 0.8434, compared with WT 0.5560 and uncertainty magnitude 0.0133. This substantial bias accompanies a qualitatively similar slowdown and less-negative moment response. The early numerical $30\degc$ lift change is sharper than the broad measured feature, and its fitted-center moment departure is window-sensitive.

At $\Gamma=45\degc$, numerical $\delta=20\degc$ lift decreases over $-6\to-4\degc$ while WT increases; the computed sequence does not reproduce the sustained measured slowdown or weak moment response convincingly. At $\delta=30\degc$, a negative moment change is present, with the strongest numerical bracket later than the measured $-4\to-2\degc$ interval. The corresponding fitted-center moment increment, 0.0168, is locally close to WT 0.0187, retaining the distinct reference constructions.

This partial correspondence bounds the interpretation: the pressure, reversal, and component analyses describe the computed load response. The symmetry-root/floor difference is a modeling limitation, but the integrated comparisons alone do not assign the discrepancies to tunnel effects or validate a measured local flow topology.

\section{Joint Numerical Loading Response}\label{sec:joint}
\subsection{Ordinary growth and reduced growth of the fixed surface}\label{sec:budget}
The reference RANS sequence shows how continuing lift growth can coexist with a substantial departure from the preceding trend (Fig.~\ref{fig:budget} and Table~\ref{tab:budget}). For I/$\Gamma=0\degc$/$\delta=20\degc$ over direct $0\to2\degc$, the fixed surface gains lift while the control loses lift, leaving a small positive total increment. The fixed-surface increase, however, is smaller than predicted by continuation of its preceding slope. Relative to the shared baseline $\{-6,-4,-2\}\degc$, both components have negative departures, with total $D_L=-0.1106$. The fixed surface therefore contributes to the lift-growth deficit even while its own lift increases.

Across three tested windows and the direct and symmetry-reflected zero-incidence solutions, the fixed fraction of total lift departure is 62.4--63.0\%. \subsection{Pressure redistribution and spatial moment weighting}\label{sec:pressure}
In the computed pressure field, upper leading-edge suction strengthens while the suction minimum near the hinge weakens at all three sampled span stations (Fig.~\ref{fig:cp}). The sections cover both physical sides through the same $0\to2\degc$ interval. These local loading increases and losses coexist; their contributions to the integrated response are quantified by the spatial load sums.

The forward region gains load more slowly than its preceding trend, whereas the intermediate region loses load (Fig.~\ref{fig:spatial}). Together, these changes reduce the expected growth of the fixed-surface contribution. The aft region also loses lift, and all three geometric regions have negative lift departures, including both regions forward of the hinge.

Geometric fore/aft increments differ slightly from the solver fixed/control increments because the hinge junction is assigned differently. In each partition, the sum of the integrated contributions includes the entire surface. Pressure supplies nearly all the principal lift departure; the small shear contribution is retained in the total, with effect-scale limits stated in Sec.~\ref{sec:analysis}.

This spatial weighting distinguishes the moment response from the force response. \subsection{Deflection contrast and reversal evolution}\label{sec:reversal}
The early I/$\delta=30\degc$ interval in Table~\ref{tab:budget} also combines positive ordinary fixed lift growth with negative fixed and control departures. Its fitted-center moment contributions compete, and the total departure changes sign across tested windows. The two deflections share the lift-budget distinction, while their moment response depends more strongly on the empirical reference trend.

Figure~\ref{fig:reversal} quantifies the numerical chordwise reversal accompanying these load changes. For I/$\delta=20\degc$, the upper-aft fraction grows from 0.146 to 0.485 over direct $0\to2\degc$, with reversal already present at $-2\degc$. For $\delta=30\degc$, it grows from 0.622 to 0.935 over $-10\to-8\degc$. Expansion accompanies reduced aft loading and the pressure-pattern change. \label{anchor:pressure_recovery}Here, pressure recovery denotes the chordwise rise downstream of the hinge suction minimum; it does not imply an attached boundary layer.

\section{Dihedral and Planform Dependence}\label{sec:geometry_effects}
\subsection{Matched dihedral: levels and incidence changes}\label{sec:dihedral}
The dihedral comparison holds Geom.~I, $\delta$, $\alpha$, $S$, and MAC fixed. Orientation and the associated root cut/domain change together. The two settings support a controlled comparison within this dataset, without interpolation to smaller dihedral angles or generalization to aircraft tails.

Measured $\delta=20\degc$ absolute lift departures are smaller with dihedral, but their relative suppression depends on the incidence interval. Over $-2\to0\degc$, the descriptive deficits relative to each preceding fitted increment are 80.6\% and 76.4\% for $\Gamma=0$ and $45\degc$; over $-2\to2\degc$, they are 64.5\% and 82.2\%. These ratios describe sampled contrasts without an assigned significance level. Lower absolute lift and weaker relative suppression are different statements.

Numerical projection accounting provides context for the absolute level difference. At $\alpha=0\degc$, $\delta=20\degc$, projection of the zero-dihedral normal load contributes about $-0.247$ of the matched lift difference $-0.301$. Changes in local normal loading and span-force projection complete the balance. This coordinate decomposition describes the level difference; it does not isolate a causal contribution to nonlinear lift growth.

For the incidence-dependent moment comparison, Fig.~\ref{fig:dihedral} instead evaluates
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal I_M=[C_{M,45}^{A_I}(\alpha_b)-C_{M,45}^{A_I}(\alpha_a)]
             -[C_{M,0}^{A_I}(\alpha_b)-C_{M,0}^{A_I}(\alpha_a)]
\label{eq:interval}
\end{equation}
with identical incidence intervals and the common numerical axis. The corresponding contrasts of the integrated $z\,dF_x$ and $-(x-x_{A_I})dF_z$ terms complete its sum. For $\delta=30\degc$ over $-2\to0\degc$, $\mathcal I_M=-0.0184$, with both terms negative. Their changing contributions across the other intervals show how geometric moment weighting enters the incidence-dependent response. These are ordinary interval contrasts without baseline subtraction.

\subsection{Second planform and scope of the result}\label{sec:planform}
For II/$\delta=20\degc$, Table~\ref{tab:budget} again shows positive ordinary fixed lift growth and negative control growth, with negative departures of both components. Its fitted-center moment departures are both positive and reinforce. A small partial cancellation about the original numerical origin is reference-specific and sensitive to baseline and choice of zero-incidence solution (Appendix~\ref{app:sensitivity}).

The second-planform result accompanies the poorly reproduced measured lift slowdown described in Sec.~\ref{sec:response}. The direct and symmetry-reflected zero-incidence solutions and their corresponding coefficients remain distinct in Fig.~\ref{fig:comparison}, with their interval sensitivity in the appendix. Since sweep, taper, aspect ratio, chord distribution, area, and MAC Reynolds number differ between I and II, the comparison describes the two tested planforms rather than an isolated sweep effect.

These results distinguish continuing integrated lift growth from reduced growth of its component contributions at fixed deflection. Moment adds the spatial weighting of those changes. A lift-only description or a model with constant slopes consequently loses information about load allocation and pitching response. The present departures vary incidence, not control deflection, and do not measure a control derivative. Their engineering interpretation is confined to the tested planforms, full-span control geometry, and conditions.

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}
The measured pre-stall response contains distinct lift and moment changes. The baseline $20\degc$ lift slowdown is pronounced, while a moment-response change is present at $10\degc$. Inclined weak moment features and the curved early $30\degc$ response require separate incidence and uncertainty descriptions.

For I/$\Gamma=0\degc$/$\delta=20\degc$, the numerical fixed surface continues gaining lift while both fixed and control contributions fall below their preceding trends. About 63\% of the total lift departure is allocated to the fixed part across the tested windows and zero-incidence solutions; the absolute departure is more sensitive to the baseline. Sectional pressures and spatial load sums show increased forward suction together with hinge/aft losses and reduced expected growth in both fixed-chord regions. The fixed and control moment departures about the fitted aerodynamic center reinforce, with the control contributing more strongly than the fixed surface. Similar aft force losses also produce larger moment changes outboard.

Matched dihedral comparisons separate absolute load projection from relative lift suppression and quantify the incidence-dependent contributions of both moment-arm terms. Both fitted-center moment departures also reinforce for the second planform. These findings describe the tested configurations; their simultaneous geometric differences limit single-parameter generalization.

The detailed load accounting applies to the computed states. Partial WT--RANS correspondence includes substantial baseline lift bias, inclined irregularities, and an inadequately reproduced second-planform lift slowdown. The existing $35\degc$ mesh study provides integrated-load sensitivity evidence at its tested conditions, while the present spatial interpretation remains bounded by those accuracy limits. The reference computed case demonstrates that continued lift growth can mask reduced growth of both surface contributions, whose different spatial weighting produces a distinct pitching-moment response.

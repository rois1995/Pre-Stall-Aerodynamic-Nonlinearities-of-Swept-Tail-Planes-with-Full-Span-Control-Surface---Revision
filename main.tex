\begin{abstract}
\label{anchor:abstract}
Local loading changes on a swept lifting surface with a deflected plain control can alter lift growth and pitching moment well before maximum lift. MONNALISA wind-tunnel measurements and Reynolds-averaged Navier--Stokes solutions are used to examine how fixed-surface and full-span control loads combine in this response. For the positive-sweep, zero-dihedral model at a $20\degc$ deflection, the computed fixed surface continues gaining lift while both surface contributions fall below their preceding incidence trends. Across the tested baseline windows and zero-incidence solutions, the fixed surface accounts for about 63\% of the total lift departure; the control contributes more strongly to the moment departure about the fitted aerodynamic center. Pressure sections and regional integrations connect these different allocations to stronger leading-edge suction, reduced hinge/aft loading, and larger outboard moment weights. Matched dihedral comparisons distinguish absolute load projection from changes with incidence. The detailed interpretation concerns the computed states, for which correspondence with measured integrated coefficients is partial. The combined force and moment analysis identifies loading changes that continuing total lift growth alone would conceal.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
A deflected trailing-edge control changes both the amount of lift generated by a lifting surface and the location at which that load acts. Its response with incidence is therefore relevant to pitching moment as well as lift, even before the maximum-lift condition is reached. When local loading changes, total lift can continue increasing while its growth slows and the pitching response changes more abruptly. Interpreting this behavior requires relating the integrated coefficients to the loads on the fixed and moving parts of the surface.

Sectional plain-flap experiments establish the starting point for this problem. Sears and Liddell~\cite{sears1942plain} measured lift, drag, pitching moment, and hinge moment for a 30\%-chord plain flap on an NACA 0015 airfoil, including sealed and unsealed hinge gaps. Separation reduced flap effectiveness at large deflections, although additional deflection still increased lift within the tested range. Their comparison with a thinner symmetric section also showed that similar lift effectiveness could coexist with different hinge-moment behavior. Reduced flap effectiveness is thus established physics; neither the presence of separation nor a single integrated lift trend fully characterizes the loading response.

On a finite wing, flap-induced loading is also shaped by the spanwise flow and planform. Campbell, Blanks, and Leaver~\cite{campbell1956fullspan} investigated rectangular wings of small aspect ratio with full-span plain flaps of several chord fractions. Their force and pitching-moment measurements, compared with thin-wing theory and empirical thickness and boundary-layer allowances, related flap effectiveness and center of pressure to flap chord and aspect ratio. Those unswept, thinner, transition-tripped models differ from the present swept models, but they establish why the location of the additional load matters alongside its magnitude.

For swept wings, Harper and Maki~\cite{harper1964sweptstall} documented how spanwise changes in local lift response can produce pitching-moment nonlinearity below maximum lift. The first appearance of local stall need not coincide with the first integrated moment change, because the force change and its moment arm vary along the span. Their broad stall review provides context for examining load redistribution, rather than an equivalent test of the present deflected-control configurations.

These relationships can enter reduced-order models through their aerodynamic inputs and corrections. Sivells and Neely~\cite{sivells1947liftingline} coupled nonlinear sectional lift data to lifting-line theory and obtained improved finite-wing predictions near maximum lift; they also calculated wing pitching moment from sectional moment data. More recently, Goitia and Llamas~\cite{goitia2019vlm} coupled a vortex-lattice formulation to viscous sectional data, with effective-sweep and finite-wing corrections, and compared nonlinear lift predictions for wing and tail geometries. Such formulations can represent nonlinear responses, although their sectional inputs and three-dimensional corrections govern what they can resolve. The relevant modeling question is therefore whether a particular formulation represents the loading changes of interest, rather than whether its nominal order excludes nonlinearity.

The immediate basis for the present work is the MONNALISA database publication~\cite{rausa2026monnalisa}. Its Sec.~IV and Fig.~11 already document the baseline pre-stall lift nonlinearity, expansion of reversed flow on the control, associated pressure changes, and continued growth of total lift. The publication also reports nonlinear responses from the DUST lifting-line formulation using sectional tables and corrections, alongside smoother potential-based panel and vortex-lattice predictions. These observations establish both the existing physical interpretation and the model-specific nature of the lower-fidelity response.

The remaining loading question is how changes on the fixed surface and control combine to produce the joint lift and moment response. Continued fixed-surface lift growth, for example, does not establish that this contribution follows its preceding trend. Comparing ordinary increments with departures from that trend identifies which components supply the reduced total growth, while spatial pressure integrations show why the component ranking can differ for moment. This study develops that quantitative interpretation from the existing RANS solutions, first assessing their integrated coefficients against the measured response and its supplied uncertainty. Changes in control deflection and geometry then test how broadly the reference loading pattern persists within the dataset.

The test articles are isolated tailplane-inspired wings with full-span, 30\%-chord plain controls; no fuselage, wing wake, or complete-aircraft trim condition is represented. The $45\degc$ dihedral setting supplies a pronounced orientation contrast on the same planform, while a second planform changes several geometric parameters together. These comparisons define the scope of the analysis without establishing behavior for partial-span controls, other flap chords, or intermediate dihedral angles.

\label{end:sec:intro}

 C_D=C_{F,z}\sin\alpha+C_{F,x}\cos\alpha,\qquad

\subsection{Numerical method and existing accuracy evidence}\label{sec:numerics}
The existing SU2~\cite{palacios2013stanford} calculations solve compressible ideal-gas RANS at the nominal WT Mach and Reynolds conditions, with Sutherland viscosity, SA turbulence closure~\cite{spalart1992sa}, and BCM transition treatment~\cite{cakmakcioglu2020bcm}. The BCM closure supplies an algebraic intermittency correction rather than an independently transported transition equation. Unstructured finite volumes use median-dual vertex control volumes. Mean-flow convection uses Roe fluxes with second-order MUSCL reconstruction~\cite{van1979towards}, Green--Gauss gradients, and a Venkatakrishnan limiter~\cite{venkatakrishnan1993accuracy} with coefficient 0.05. SA convection uses scalar upwinding without MUSCL, hence first-order reconstruction. The stated spatial orders therefore apply separately to the mean-flow and SA equations.

\label{anchor:wall_model}The surfaces have no-slip, zero-heat-flux boundaries; the exterior uses a far-field boundary and the root plane a symmetry boundary. This excludes the WT floor, its layer, and the support. Implicit Euler pseudo-time advancement, FGMRES linear solves, adaptive CFL, and staged restarts were used to obtain steady states. The configured Cauchy stopping criterion monitors lift and drag over 100 entries, commonly at $3\times10^{-6}$ and at $10^{-6}$ in later Geom.~II stages. Moment is not a stopping quantity; iteration caps and restarts also occur.

The saved surface states are checked against their force reports and terminal histories. Matches are established for 41 of the 44 interpreted states; the exceptions and incomplete final-stage reconstruction-order registration are documented in Appendix~\ref{app:sensitivity}. These checks establish the available state correspondence, while the grid assessment and measured coefficients address distinct aspects of numerical accuracy.

The automated mesh procedure~\cite{rausa2026monnalisa} uses anisotropic surface triangles, leading-edge and hinge/junction refinement, a hex-dominant octree volume, and incidence-oriented wake refinement. Nominal controls are a $2.5\degc$ leading-edge angle, $1\degc$ junction angle, seven trailing-edge points, maximum surface spacing 0.02~m, and boundary-layer growth ratio 1.15. Actual first-cell height and layer count are not registered for every retained mesh. Representative stored $y^+$ medians are 0.26 for I/$\delta=20\degc$/$\alpha=2\degc$, 0.19 for I/$\Gamma=45\degc$/$\delta=30\degc$/$\alpha=0\degc$, and 0.23 for II/$\delta=20\degc$/$\alpha=0\degc$, with maxima approximately 1.22, 1.26, and 1.13. These support resolved near-wall treatment for those samples.

Selected surface meshes have 122,860 points/182,423 elements for I/$\delta=20\degc$ at $0,2,4\degc$, 141,828/209,641 for I/$\Gamma=45\degc$/$\delta=30\degc$ at $-2,0,2\degc$, and 105,506/156,333 for II/$\delta=20\degc$ at $0,2,4\degc$. These are surface counts. The retained volume for the I/$\alpha=0\degc$/$\delta=-20\degc$ parent contains 9,440,030 points and 18,918,212 volume elements; equivalent registered volume counts are not available for every interpreted state.

The nominal mesh level was selected for the extensive MONNALISA campaign, balancing spatial resolution with the computational demands of covering multiple geometries, incidences, and control-surface deflections. The choice was informed by the four-level lift/drag refinement assessment in Ref.~\cite{rausa2026monnalisa}, summarized in Table~\ref{tab:mesh} and Fig.~\ref{fig:mesh}. For Geom.~I at $\delta=35\degc$, $\Gamma=0,45\degc$, and $\alpha=0,18\degc$, the nominal grids contain approximately 9.7--12.7 million points, compared with 50.1--65.9 million on the finest level. Nominal-to-finest differences are 1.20--2.14\% for lift and 1.13--2.49\% for drag. This evidence informed the nominal-level production choice while maintaining broad parameter-space coverage. Pitching moments subsequently recovered from the same existing calculations add a condition-dependent sensitivity assessment: the $C_M^O$ difference at $\Gamma=0\degc$, $\alpha=18\degc$ is 0.01343, and the inclined sequence is nonmonotonic.

The assessment characterizes integrated-load sensitivity at these database conditions; it does not quantify spatial-discretization uncertainty in the local pre-stall flow or component contributions. The experimental comparison in Sec.~\ref{sec:response} provides a complementary assessment: the nominal-grid reference calculation reproduces a lift-slope reduction and a change toward less-negative pitching moment, with a displaced lift interval and differences in coefficient level.

\subsection{Additive loading and empirical departures}\label{sec:analysis}
A total lift increase can conceal reduced growth of one or more surface contributions. The analysis therefore compares the actual coefficient change with the change obtained by continuing an empirical preceding trend, then resolves both measures into additive loads. Solver surface groups define the fixed surface (upper/lower fixed panels), the control (upper/lower control panels, hinge junction, and blunt trailing edge), and tip. Pressure and viscous contributions are retained on both physical sides with the same global $S$ and $\bar c$. Tip is included in every total, and all component coefficients are displayed at their actual magnitude.

For coefficient $Q$ and component $j$, the ordinary increment measures the loading change between the two sampled incidences. Subtracting the continuation of its preceding slope gives the departure,
\begin{equation}
 \Delta C_{Q,j}=C_{Q,j}(\alpha_b)-C_{Q,j}(\alpha_a),\qquad
 D_{Q,j}=\Delta C_{Q,j}-m_{Q,j}^{\mathrm{pre}}(\alpha_b-\alpha_a).
\label{eq:departure}
\end{equation}
The slope is an unweighted linear least-squares fit to the declared preceding samples. Using identical samples and weights for every component and the total preserves additivity for both increments and departures. The primary $\delta=20\degc$ baseline is $\{-6,-4,-2\}\degc$; the early I/$\delta=30\degc$ numerical baseline is $\{-16,-14,-12\}\degc$. These are empirical references, with no assumption that the preceding flow is attached. Adjacent windows and the direct and symmetry-reflected zero-incidence solutions test the dependence of the result on that reference. Where the preceding sequence is curved, signed finite differences describe the response without imposing a unique event amplitude.

Measured departures must also be assessed against the supplied uncertainty. For WT contrasts, Eq.~\ref{eq:departure} is written as $D=\sum_i w_iC_i$, combining repeated uses of any sample into one weight. The two reported sensitivities are
\begin{equation}
 u_{\mathrm{ind}}=\left[\sum_i(w_i u_i)^2\right]^{1/2},\qquad
 u_{\mathrm{L1}}=\sum_i|w_i|u_i.
\label{eq:uncertainty}
\end{equation}
The first is conditional on independence; the second bounds a perturbation when each sample varies within its supplied magnitude. Neither is an established confidence interval or a new experimental uncertainty budget. Covariance-dependent significance is not inferred.

The component budgets identify which surface contributes, but pressure integration is needed to locate the changes within it. Saved nodal $C_p$ and vector $\mathbf C_f$ are therefore integrated over the complete triangulated surface. Linear traction and position-times-traction are integrated exactly within each triangle, with
\begin{equation}
 C_M(x_A)=\frac{1}{q_\infty S\bar c}
 \int\big[z\,dF_x-(x-x_A)dF_z\big].
\label{eq:moment}
\end{equation}
The two terms are retained separately where a common-axis dihedral comparison needs them. All 44 full-surface reconstructions have endpoint residuals below $1.5\times10^{-4}$. Maximum lift and moment residuals are $2.71\times10^{-5}$ and $7.06\times10^{-5}$. Conservative absolute-weight residual bounds are checked separately against each increment or departure, with a 10\% effect-scale limit for an adopted interpretation. For the baseline lift departure, the bound is $1.01\times10^{-5}$ against $|D_L|=0.11060$. Small shear changes that fail their corresponding bound are not assigned a resolved mechanism. Accounting closure and reconstruction agreement are distinct from iteration, mesh sensitivity, and experiment--model discrepancy; global residuals do not bound each regional error.

Geometric bins use $\eta=s/b$ and straightened $\xi$, obtained by undoing the control rotation about the three-dimensional hinge. They include both sides and root extensions; end faces are retained separately. Whole triangles are assigned by centroid. These geometric regions differ from the solver surface groups, especially in their assignment of the hinge junction. Sectional pressure curves use $\eta=0.2,0.5,0.8$ and preserve actual contour connectivity on the native upper/lower fixed and control panels, excluding junction and end faces. Three sections illustrate pressure changes; complete integrations supply the budgets.

\label{anchor:reversal_definition}A chordwise reversal indicator measures the actual upper geometric-aft area ($\xi\geq0.7$, excluding end caps) on which $\mathbf C_f\cdot\mathbf t_c<-\epsilon$, with downstream chord direction projected into the face tangent. Linear nodal signs are clipped within triangles. The fraction $f_{\mathrm{rev}}$ is tested at $\epsilon=0,10^{-6},10^{-5}$. It measures a specified component of reversed shear, rather than the full three-dimensional separation footprint.

Both departures exceed the stated uncertainty sensitivities (Table~\ref{tab:wt}); the corresponding pointwise lift- and moment-coefficient uncertainty magnitudes are approximately 0.0127--0.0133 and 0.0046--0.0047. These sampled contrasts identify the response change at the available incidence spacing.

The lower-deflection result shows why the two coefficients should be examined together. At $10\degc$, the measured moment increment changes from $-0.0038$ over $-2\to0\degc$ to $+0.0113$ over $0\to2\degc$, so a moment-response change is present below the deflection producing the pronounced $20\degc$ lift slowdown.

Increasing deflection to $30\degc$ instead gives a broader early feature: lift increments are 0.0765, 0.0642, and 0.1113 over $-12\to-10$, $-10\to-8$, and $-8\to-6\degc$, while moment already increases by about 0.0180 and 0.0183 on the first two intervals. Curvature in the preceding lift sequence makes its departure window-sensitive, spanning both signs. Local increments therefore describe this feature more directly than one departure amplitude.

Changing orientation on the same planform does not simply shift this joint response. For I/$\Gamma=45\degc$/$\delta=20\degc$, measured lift growth remains suppressed across $-2\to2\degc$, but the positive moment increment over $-2\to0\degc$ has a departure smaller than both uncertainty sensitivities. At $\delta=30\degc$, the selected lift slowdown occurs over $-2\to0\degc$, whereas moment decreases earlier, by 0.0265 over $-4\to-2\degc$. That moment departure is comparable with $u_{\mathrm{ind}}$ and smaller than $u_{\mathrm{L1}}$. Thus coincident, clearly resolved lift and moment events cannot be assumed for the inclined model.

The second planform provides a further contrast in the joint response. For II/$\delta=20\degc$, measured lift grows by 0.0440 over $0\to2\degc$, compared with approximately 0.0921 on the preceding and following intervals, and moment increases by 0.0187. The primary moment departure exceeds both sensitivities, although adjacent-window departures do not uniformly exceed the primary L1 magnitude. Its lift and moment changes are locally evident, with the $30\degc$ response also shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:comparison} for context.

\label{anchor:comparison}To assess what the surface fields can explain, the numerical response is compared separately for lift and moment. For I/$\delta=20\degc$, the RANS lift slowdown occurs over $0\to2\degc$, one sampled interval later than in WT. At zero incidence the direct and reflected numerical lift coefficients are approximately 0.8422 and 0.8434, compared with WT 0.5560 and uncertainty magnitude 0.0133. A qualitatively similar slowdown and less-negative moment response therefore coexist with substantial lift-level bias. At $30\degc$, the early numerical lift change is sharper than the broad measured feature, and its fitted-center moment departure is window-sensitive.

At $\Gamma=45\degc$, numerical $\delta=20\degc$ lift decreases over $-6\to-4\degc$ while WT increases; the computed sequence does not reproduce the sustained measured slowdown or weak moment response convincingly. At $\delta=30\degc$, a negative moment change is present, with the strongest numerical bracket later than the measured $-4\to-2\degc$ interval. For II/$\delta=20\degc$, the direct numerical $0\to2\degc$ lift increment is 0.0935, more than twice WT 0.0440. The corresponding fitted-center moment increment, 0.0168, is locally close to WT 0.0187, retaining the distinct reference constructions.

The reference calculation captures the direction of the joint response, but the other comparisons show that this correspondence is not uniform. The following analysis therefore asks how the computed component loads produce their own integrated lift and moment changes, using pressure and reversal as supporting numerical evidence. It does not validate a measured local topology. The symmetry-root/floor distinction remains a modeling limitation, without the integrated comparisons assigning the discrepancies to tunnel effects.

\section{Joint Numerical Loading Response}\label{sec:joint}
\subsection{Ordinary growth and reduced growth of the fixed surface}\label{sec:budget}
For the reference RANS sequence, the continuing total lift increase is supplied by a gain on the fixed surface that outweighs a loss on the control (Fig.~\ref{fig:budget} and Table~\ref{tab:budget}). This occurs for I/$\Gamma=0\degc$/$\delta=20\degc$ over direct $0\to2\degc$. The positive fixed-surface increment, however, is smaller than continuation of its preceding slope would give. Relative to the shared baseline $\{-6,-4,-2\}\degc$, both components have negative departures and the total is $D_L=-0.1106$. The reduction in total lift growth consequently involves the fixed surface as well as the control, despite their opposite ordinary increments.

Moment about the fitted aerodynamic center gives a different component ranking. Both moment departures are positive, and the control contributes more strongly (Table~\ref{tab:budget}). Thus the fixed surface supplies the larger lift-growth deficit while the control supplies the larger moment departure, even though both are formed from the same loading states. The difference motivates examination of where the pressure changes occur and how they are weighted in moment.

The fixed-surface share of the lift departure remains 62.4--63.0\% across three tested baseline windows and the direct and symmetry-reflected zero-incidence solutions. The direct-state total varies more, from $-0.1368$ to $-0.1050$, because the earliest baseline has appreciable curvature. About 63\% therefore describes an allocation relative to these empirical trends: its stability supports the component ranking within the tested definitions, rather than establishing an experimental component fraction or causal share.

\subsection{Pressure redistribution and spatial moment weighting}\label{sec:pressure}
The different lift and moment rankings can be examined in the pressure distributions at the same two incidences used in the budget. At all three span stations, upper leading-edge suction strengthens while the suction minimum near the hinge weakens (Fig.~\ref{fig:cp}). This combination is consistent with positive fixed-surface lift growth and loss of control lift over $0\to2\degc$. \label{anchor:pressure_recovery}Here, pressure recovery denotes the chordwise rise downstream of the hinge suction minimum; it does not imply an attached boundary layer. These two-state sections show ordinary pressure changes. Establishing which regions fall below their preceding loading trends additionally requires the baseline-state integrals.

Sections follow the upper/lower fixed and control panels, with connectivity and face exclusions defined in Sec.~\ref{sec:analysis}. The regional integrations provide that distinction (Fig.~\ref{fig:spatial}). The forward region still gains lift, but less than its preceding trend, while the intermediate region loses lift. Both regions forward of the hinge therefore contribute to the fixed-surface departure. The aft region also loses lift, giving negative departures in all three chordwise regions. A stronger leading-edge suction peak is thus compatible with reduced overall fixed-surface lift growth when the rest of its loading and its preceding trend are considered.

Geometric fore/aft increments differ slightly from the solver fixed/control increments because the hinge junction is assigned differently. In each partition, the sum of the integrated contributions includes the entire surface. Pressure supplies nearly all the principal lift departure; the small shear contribution is retained in the total, with effect-scale limits stated in Sec.~\ref{sec:analysis}.

Comparing the aft regions along the span then explains why their moment contribution differs from their lift contribution. Similar aft lift losses produce moment increments about the fitted aerodynamic center that increase from about 0.0025 inboard to 0.0091 outboard. The force changes have different spatial weights in the moment integral, consistent with the importance of load location in finite-wing pitching response~\cite{harper1964sweptstall}. Here the evidence is the integrated regional change, rather than a continuous load density; Appendix~\ref{app:sensitivity} tests the reference and partition dependence.

\label{end:sec:pressure}
\subsection{Deflection contrast and reversal evolution}\label{sec:reversal}
Increasing control deflection tests whether the reference distinction between ordinary growth and reduced growth persists in the earlier nonlinear interval. The I/$\delta=30\degc$ results in Table~\ref{tab:budget} again combine positive ordinary fixed lift growth with negative fixed and control departures. Its fitted-center moment contributions compete, however, and the total departure changes sign across tested windows. The lift-budget pattern extends to this deflection, while its moment response is more sensitive to the empirical preceding trend.

The associated reversal measure helps relate this budget to the changing aft pressure and loading. For I/$\delta=20\degc$, the upper-aft chordwise reversal fraction grows from 0.146 to 0.485 over direct $0\to2\degc$, with reversal already present at $-2\degc$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:reversal}). At $\delta=30\degc$, it grows from 0.622 to 0.935 over $-10\to-8\degc$. Expansion of an existing reversed-shear region accompanies reduced aft loading; the indicator describes this association without equating its area to every form of three-dimensional separation.

The inclined solutions show why reversal area alone is insufficient to explain the integrated response. For I/$\Gamma=45\degc$/$\delta=30\degc$, the control is already extensively reversed over the selected moment interval. At later incidences the stored states are nonmonotonic: between $2$ and $4\degc$, the fraction falls from 0.579 to 0.090 at $\delta=20\degc$ and from 0.912 to 0.203 at $\delta=30\degc$. The available steady samples do not establish the origin of these changes. They also contain a distinct leading-edge reversed-shear region, for which the local transition-model fields give additional state information.

In the inclined $\delta=30\degc$, $\alpha=2\degc$ solution, this region is on the upper outboard leading edge ($2/3\leq\eta\leq0.95$, $0\leq\xi\leq0.15$). \label{anchor:local_BCM}Modeled BCM intermittency on the reversing-centroid subset has median $2.4\times10^{-5}$ and maximum $8.6\times10^{-5}$, with zero wall eddy viscosity and SA working variable. The low wall-model values characterize this particular stored feature alongside the aft reversal evolution. They do not provide a laminar/turbulent boundary-layer profile or establish a laminar separation bubble.

\section{Dihedral and Planform Dependence}\label{sec:geometry_effects}
\subsection{Matched dihedral: levels and incidence changes}\label{sec:dihedral}
The dihedral comparison examines how changing the spatial orientation of the same planform affects the joint loading response. Geom.~I, $\delta$, $\alpha$, $S$, and MAC are held fixed; orientation and the associated root cut/domain change together. It therefore offers a controlled geometric contrast within the dataset, without supplying intermediate-angle or aircraft-level predictions.

The measurements first distinguish the size of the lift departure from its size relative to each model's preceding growth. At $\delta=20\degc$, absolute departures are smaller with dihedral, but over $-2\to0\degc$ the descriptive relative deficits are 80.6\% and 76.4\% for $\Gamma=0$ and $45\degc$, whereas over $-2\to2\degc$ they are 64.5\% and 82.2\%. These sampled ratios have no assigned significance level. A smaller absolute departure consequently does not imply uniformly weaker relative suppression.

The numerical force decomposition addresses the absolute level change before considering its evolution with incidence. At $\alpha=0\degc$, $\delta=20\degc$, projection of the zero-dihedral normal load contributes about $-0.247$ of the matched lift difference $-0.301$. Changed local normal loading and span-force projection complete the balance. This coordinate accounting distinguishes projection from changed forces in the level difference, without isolating a causal contribution to nonlinear lift growth.

Moment requires a further comparison because dihedral changes both loading and its spatial weights. To examine how the moment evolves over the same incidence interval, rather than only comparing its absolute level, both numerical configurations are referred to the common axis $A_I$. Figure~\ref{fig:dihedral} evaluates
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal I_M=[C_{M,45}^{A_I}(\alpha_b)-C_{M,45}^{A_I}(\alpha_a)]
             -[C_{M,0}^{A_I}(\alpha_b)-C_{M,0}^{A_I}(\alpha_a)]
\label{eq:interval}
\end{equation}
with identical incidence intervals. Contrasts of the integrated $z\,dF_x$ and $-(x-x_{A_I})dF_z$ terms complete this ordinary-increment difference. For $\delta=30\degc$ over $-2\to0\degc$, $\mathcal I_M=-0.0184$, with both terms negative. Their changing contributions across the other intervals show how force changes and spatial moment weighting enter the incidence-dependent response. This comparison uses no baseline subtraction and does not separate geometry from its effect on the computed flow.

\subsection{Second planform and scope of the result}\label{sec:planform}
The second planform tests whether the component relationship found for Geom.~I also occurs with a different sweep, taper, aspect ratio, and chord distribution. For II/$\delta=20\degc$, Table~\ref{tab:budget} again shows positive ordinary fixed lift growth and negative control growth, with negative departures of both components. Its fitted-center moment departures are both positive and reinforce. A small partial cancellation about the original numerical origin has a different meaning: it is reference-specific and sensitive to baseline and zero-incidence choice (Appendix~\ref{app:sensitivity}). It therefore does not replace the fitted-center interpretation.

This recurrence is a result for the computed planform, whose measured lift slowdown is poorly reproduced (Sec.~\ref{sec:response}). The direct and symmetry-reflected zero-incidence solutions remain distinct in Fig.~\ref{fig:comparison}, with their corresponding interval sensitivity in the appendix. Sweep, taper, aspect ratio, chord distribution, area, and MAC Reynolds number all differ between I and II. The comparison accordingly extends the component analysis to another planform without identifying an isolated sweep effect.

For the tested incidence response, the useful information is the combination of continued integrated lift growth, reduced growth of component loads, and their different moment weights. A lift curve or constant-slope description alone does not supply this allocation. Nonlinear models can admit loading changes through sectional data and corrections, as discussed in Sec.~\ref{sec:intro}; the present analysis supplies a spatially resolved account for the RANS states rather than a general verdict on reduced-order capability. The departures vary incidence at fixed deflection, not control deflection, and therefore do not measure a control derivative. Their engineering scope is the tested planforms, full-span control geometry, and conditions.

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}
The measured pre-stall behavior is a joint lift and pitching-moment response whose features need not appear at the same deflection or over the same incidence interval. Geom.~I at zero dihedral exhibits a pronounced $20\degc$ lift slowdown, while a moment-response change is already present at $10\degc$. The early $30\degc$ lift response is broader and its departure is window-sensitive, while the inclined moment features are not clearly resolved above both uncertainty sensitivities.

The reference numerical budget shows why continuing lift growth is compatible with a substantial loading departure. For I/$\Gamma=0\degc$/$\delta=20\degc$, fixed-surface lift still increases, but both fixed and control loads fall below their preceding trends. About 63\% of the total lift departure is allocated to the fixed part across the tested windows and zero-incidence solutions, while its absolute amplitude is more baseline-sensitive. Increased leading-edge suction coexists with hinge/aft losses; regional integrations establish reduced expected growth in both fixed-chord regions. About the fitted aerodynamic center, the two moment departures reinforce and the control contributes more strongly. Similar aft lift losses also produce larger moment changes outboard, linking the different component rankings to spatial loading.

Changing orientation distinguishes lower absolute load from weaker relative suppression. The matched dihedral analysis quantifies load projection at one incidence and the changing contributions of both moment-arm terms over common incidence intervals. The second planform also has reinforcing fitted-center moment departures, extending the component comparison without isolating its simultaneous geometric changes.

These interpretations describe the computed states. Their partial experimental correspondence includes substantial reference lift bias, inclined irregularities, and an inadequately reproduced second-planform lift slowdown. Future computations on the finer levels of the established mesh hierarchy are planned to assess the resolution sensitivity of the pre-stall load redistribution, with particular attention to pitching moment and the fixed- and control-surface contributions. In the reference computed case, fixed-surface lift growth can mask that surface's substantial contribution to reduced total growth, while the control supplies the larger departure in pitching moment about the stated reference.

\subsection{Moment-reference effects}
At the original numerical origin, the baseline total moment departure is $+0.10028$, with fixed $+0.05286$ and control $+0.04743$.
